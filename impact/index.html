Where the Numbers
Decide What Happens

Listing decisions, harvest plans, and recovery strategies all rest on population estimates. We work on the species where those estimates are hardest to produce.

Why This Work Matters

Rigorous Counts, Consequential Species

Northern spotted owls, Humboldt martens, fishers, wolverines — the animals at the center of Pacific Northwest conservation are rare, wide-ranging, nocturnal, and averse to being found. They are, in other words, exactly the species for which conventional survey methods produce the least reliable answers.

That is a problem, because the answers matter enormously. A density estimate determines whether a population is declining or merely undersampled. A detection map shapes where harvest is permitted. An occupancy trend informs whether a species is listed, downlisted, or left unprotected. When the estimate is uncertain, the decision built on it inherits that uncertainty.

Our role is not to argue for a particular outcome. It is to make the underlying numbers good enough that the argument can be had honestly — combining noninvasive genetics, AI-assisted detection, and spatial capture-recapture to produce estimates with defensible confidence intervals, published in the peer-reviewed literature and documented to regulatory standards.
