Regression and causal inference

  • GLM / GLMM with biological interpretation: One response measured on sampling units (sites, plots, nests, animals, quadrats, surveys) and the question is how it changes with habitat, environmental or treatment predictors…
  • Causal effects from observational data (DAG + g-computation): The question is causal ("does canopy cover increase trout?", "what would happen to native plants if pastures were not grazed?") and the data are observational (field surveys, monitoring).
  • Structural equation models (piecewise SEM and lavaan): Several measured variables on the same sampling units (plots, sites, lakes, animals) are thought to influence each other as a network (e.g. fire -> canopy -> shrubs -> birds)…

Occupancy

  • Single-species occupancy (static single-season and dynamic multi-season): Detection/non-detection surveys repeated at sites (camera traps, call surveys, eDNA replicates, point counts reduced to 0/1)…
  • Multispecies occupancy with species interactions (Rota et al. 2016): Detection/non-detection surveys of 2 or 3 species at the same sites (camera traps, call surveys, eDNA, point counts reduced to 0/1), repeated so detection can be estimated…
  • Two-species conditional occupancy (dominant/subordinate; Richmond et al. 2010, Waddle et al. 2010): Repeated detection/non-detection surveys of TWO species at the same sites, where one species can reasonably be treated as dominant (predator, invasive competitor…
  • Spatial, multi-species and integrated occupancy (spOccupancy): Detection/non-detection surveys repeated at sites with known coordinates, when (a) occupancy is likely spatially clustered beyond what the covariates explain (single species, spPGOcc)…
  • Abundance-mediated species interactions (dominant abundance -> subordinate occupancy): Camera-trap (or other repeated detection/non-detection) surveys of two species where one (dominant, e.g. a predator or competitor) may affect where the other (subordinate) occurs…

Abundance

  • N-mixture models for repeated counts (closed and open): The same sites (points, plots, transects, stream reaches) were counted two or more times within a period when the number of animals there did not change…
  • Distance sampling (conventional, multiple-covariate and hierarchical): Observers walked line transects or stood at points and recorded the distance (perpendicular for lines, radial for points) to every animal or group they detected…

Spatial capture-recapture

  • Spatial capture-recapture (closed population density): Individually identified animals detected at fixed detectors over repeated occasions within one closed season (camera traps of marked/spotted species, DNA from hair snares…
  • Spatial mark-resight (SMR) and generalized spatial mark-resight (gSMR): Only part of the population carries individual marks (collars, ear tags, natural marks on a subset) and cameras (or other resighting detectors) photograph both marked and unmarked animals…
  • Search-encounter SCR (scat-detection dogs, snow-tracking, transect searches for genetic samples): Individuals identified from noninvasive genetic samples (scats, hair, feathers, urine in snow) found along recorded search routes (GPS tracks of detection-dog teams, snow-tracking…
  • Open-population spatial capture-recapture (Jolly-Seber SCR): Individually identified animals (hair-snare or scat DNA, camera traps of marked/spotted species, live traps) detected at fixed detectors over repeated occasions in each of several seasons or years…

Demography and population dynamics

  • Cormack-Jolly-Seber apparent survival: Individually marked animals (bands, PIT tags, photo-ID, genetic tags) recaptured or resighted on repeated occasions; the question is how many survive from one occasion to the next and whether…
  • Multistate and multievent capture-recapture (Arnason-Schwarz / Pradel multievent): Individually marked animals are caught or resighted on repeated occasions and each capture records a state (site or wetland, breeding vs non-breeding, infected vs uninfected, size class)…
  • Jolly-Seber open-population capture-recapture (abundance, survival, recruitment): Individually marked animals (tags, PIT tags, bands, photo-ID, genetic ID) captured on 3+ occasions (usually years or seasons) in an open population…
  • Integrated population model (counts + capture-recapture + productivity): A local population studied for several years (>= 8, ideally 12+) with three kinds of data on the same population (annual counts of breeders…
  • Population time series - trends, density dependence and drivers of growth (state-space models): One count or abundance index per year (aerial/ground counts, nest counts, redd counts, CPUE, BBS-style route totals) for one population or several sub-populations, 10+ years…
  • Population viability analysis - count-based (diffusion / state-space) and matrix-model PVA: The question is extinction or quasi-extinction RISK - "how likely is this population to fall below N animals within 10, 25 or 50 years…

Distribution and community

  • Species distribution from presence-only records (point process, Maxent, integrated with surveys): You have presence-only records of a species (museum/herbarium specimens, iNaturalist/eBird-style incidental sightings, agency incidental reports, trapper/hunter reports) with coordinates…
  • Joint species distribution model (HMSC): Presence/absence or counts of many species (about 5-60) recorded at the same sampling units (plots, point-count stations, traps, lakes)…
  • Community composition - ordination, RDA and variation partitioning, PERMANOVA with dispersion test, beta-diversity partitioning, indicator species: A site x species table (counts, cover, biomass or presence/absence) from many sites plus a site table of environmental variables and/or groups (habitat types, treatments, regions, years)…

Movement

  • Hidden Markov models for animal movement (behavioural states): GPS / Argos-quality-filtered telemetry with fixes at a (roughly) regular interval, and the question is which movement modes an animal switches between (e.g. encamped/foraging vs travelling)…
  • Habitat selection from telemetry - integrated step selection (iSSA), RSF and home ranges: GPS (or other regular-interval) telemetry from several animals plus habitat rasters; the question is which habitats animals select or avoid while moving, how strongly (relative selection strength)…

Conservation genetics

  • Genetic diversity and population structure (FST, FIS, isolation by distance, DAPC): Multilocus genotypes (microsatellites, or SNPs coded 0/1/2 or in a VCF) from individuals sampled in several populations or sites…
  • Effective population size and relatedness: Genotypes (microsatellites or SNPs) from one or more samples of a population; question is how large the genetically effective population is (Ne), how fast it is losing diversity…

Fisheries

  • Fish growth (von Bertalanffy) and length-weight condition: Individual age-length data (otolith/scale ages) for one or more groups (populations, lakes, sexes, years); question is how fast fish grow, how large they get, at what age they approach maximum size…
  • Fish stock assessment - catch curves, stock-recruitment and surplus production: Fisheries questions about mortality, recruitment or stock status. (1) catch_curve - aged fish from a survey or fishery sample…
