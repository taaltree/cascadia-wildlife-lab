Occupancy

Spatial, multi-species and integrated occupancy (spOccupancy)

Where does the species (or each species of a community) occur, which habitat covariates are associated with occurrence, and is there residual spatial clustering, after accounting for imperfect detection?

module id: occu-spatial✓ Example verdict: DONE

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Detection/non-detection surveys repeated at sites with known coordinates, when (a) occupancy is likely spatially clustered beyond what the covariates explain (single species, spPGOcc), (b) several species were recorded at the same sites and community-level responses or species richness are wanted (msPGOcc, sfMsPGOcc), or (c) the same species was surveyed with two or more methods (point counts + ARUs, cameras + sign surveys) that should be analysed together (intPGOcc).

Not for

Single-species non-spatial analyses with likelihood output or dynamic (multi-season) occupancy (use occu-single); species interactions (occu-rota / occu-waddle); counts or abundance (N-mixture); estimating the spatial range itself precisely (weakly identified, see Known limits); presence-only data (sdm-presence-only); false-positive detections.

The data you need

Long detection table (site, survey, detected 0/1 [, species] [, method/source] [, survey covariates]); site table with projected x/y coordinates (m or km) and site covariates; optional prediction grid (x, y, same covariates).

Try it

The worked example

Fisher occupancy, canopy cover and elevation with residual spatial clustering (simulated camera-trap survey, 160 stations)

The question: Is fisher occupancy associated with canopy cover and elevation, allowing for missed detections and for residual spatial clustering, and where in the 30 x 30 km block are fishers likely to occur?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have fisher camera-trap data from 160 stations in the western Cascades, summer 2024, up to 6 weekly occasions per camera (some cameras died early). fisher_detections.csv has one row per station-week (station, week, start date, days the camera worked, detected 1/0). camera_stations.csv has UTM coordinates, canopy cover (%), elevation (m) and ownership. prediction_grid_1km.csv is a 1-km grid with the same covariates. Is fisher occupancy related to canopy cover and elevation? Please account for fishers we missed and for spatial clustering, and make a map of predicted occupancy. I need results and figures for a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Occupancy probability at average covariate values and zero spatial effect is 0.50 (95% CrI 0.36 to 0.65).
  • Averaged over the 160 surveyed sites, mean occupancy probability is 0.51 (95% CrI 0.43 to 0.59), and the estimated proportion of surveyed sites that are occupied is 0.51 (95% CrI 0.46 to 0.57), compared with 0.44 of sites where the species was detected at least once (the difference is the correction for missed detections).
  • Occupancy and Canopy cover (%): each 1-SD increase (1 SD = 12.9 in original units) multiplies the odds of occupancy by 2.75 (95% CrI 1.65 to 5.26); predicted occupancy goes from 0.28 at 1 SD below the mean to 0.73 at 1 SD above it (other covariates at their means). Per 10-unit increase the odds of occupancy are multiplied by 2.19 (95% CrI 1.48 to 3.64).
  • Occupancy and Elevation (m): each 1-SD increase (1 SD = 246 in original units) multiplies the odds of occupancy by 0.58 (95% CrI 0.32 to 0.97); predicted occupancy goes from 0.63 at 1 SD below the mean to 0.37 at 1 SD above it. Per 200-unit increase the odds of occupancy are multiplied by 0.64 (95% CrI 0.40 to 0.98).
  • Detection: when the species is present, a single survey detects it with probability 0.30 (95% CrI 0.25 to 0.35) (at average survey conditions).
  • Detection and Days camera active in the week: per 1-unit increase the odds of detection are multiplied by 1.48 (95% CrI 1.12 to 2.09). This is a detection effect (how easily the species is found), not a habitat effect.
  • The residual spatial variance is 0.95 (95% CrI 0.17 to 3.89) on the logit scale; its prior alone gives a 95% interval of 0.18 to 4.13, so the data add little information: these numbers mostly restate the prior and do not show whether residual clustering is strong or weak.
  • Model check: posterior predictive p-values (Freeman-Tukey, by site) are 0.55; no sign of lack of fit.
  • All effects are associations from observational data, not causal effects.

Limits that matter

  • Spatial variance and effective range are not recoverable at this design (+48% and +21% bias in planted truth) and their posteriors restate the priors; read the spatial maps as residual smoothing, not a measured clustering scale.
  • Occupancy slopes lean away from zero at modest sample sizes (+8% canopy, +11.5% elevation in planted truth; 10-28% at 100-120 sites in VALIDATION.md; ~10% inflation if no real clustering). Trust direction; report the non-spatial PGOcc fit alongside (canopy 0.87 vs 1.01 per SD).
  • Low power for the elevation effect: an effect of -0.40 per SD was detected in only 33% of replicates; the real-data interval only just excludes zero.
  • Spatial confounding: smooth covariates such as elevation (15-km range) can have part of their effect absorbed by the spatial term (VALIDATION.md: -20% for a 90%-spatial covariate).
  • Recovery simulator differs from the example generator: independent covariates, missing weeks at random rather than camera failures, standard-normal days_active.
  • Closure over the season, no false positives and no unmodelled detection heterogeneity are assumed; stations 1 km apart may share individuals, so occupancy is better read as use.
  • Core gate caveat: the '>= 90% converged' clause cannot fail for non-convergence; convergence (100/100) was counted by hand.

How this module was validated

  • 100 simulated data sets per design with spOccupancy (3 chains x 15000 iterations, 5000 burn-in); at the default design all covariate, occupancy and detection quantities passed, while the spatial range is only descriptive.
  • Harder designs: 100 sites x 3 surveys with detection 0.25 failed on occupancy (+13.3%); community models passed on means but community SDs were biased +15-30%; one spatial factor community model converged in only 76 of 100 runs.
  • Negative control: fitting a non-spatial model to strongly spatial data biased the forest slope -27.5% (coverage 0.74) and failed the gate; false-positive rates were 0.05-0.07.
  • Cross-checks: the simulator matched spOccupancy::simOcc; the non-spatial engine matched unmarked (correlation at least 0.999); a community model matched a hand-written Nimble model.
  • Power for a forest effect of 0.8: 0.34, 0.74, 0.90 and 1.00 at 50, 100, 200 and 400 sites.
  • A separate verifier with its own simulator found a 120-site failure (slopes -24% and +28%) that disappeared at 480 sites, showed the spatial model inflates slopes on non-spatial data, reproduced the builder's runs exactly, and fixed several silent data problems.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

Status: VERIFIED WITH LIMITS for spPGOcc (default design), msPGOcc community means and intPGOcc. Not gate-passing: sfMsPGOcc at fast settings (convergence), community SDs (bias), spPGOcc at small or low-detection designs (bias), and the spatial range. spIntPGOcc is not validated.

All recovery runs use fast_settings (3 chains x 15000 iterations, burn-in 5000, thin 10, 10 neighbours, converge_on = "core"). They are gated with the unchanged core/R/recovery.R thresholds:

bias<= max(10% of truth, 3 MC SE); coverage >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (0.885 at nsim 100); >= 90% of reps converged.

Gate caveat (core bug, not fixed here): summarise_recovery() sets n_ok to the number of converged reps, so n_converged / (n_ok + n_failed) is always 1 unless a rep errored. The ">= 90% converged" clause therefore never fails for non-convergence. The convergence fractions below were counted by hand from the reps files.

2. Independent verification (2026-10-19, separate verifier agent)
What was checked

  • Literature and package source. I checked spOccupancy 0.8.2 (installed source):

    • coefficient priors N(0, var 2.72) and sigma^2 ~ IG(2, 1) match the module;
    • phi ~ U(3/max d, 3/min d) is the package default, while the module uses the 5% distance quantile for the lower range bound (deliberate and documented);
    • the exponential covariance is sigma^2 exp(-phi d), so effective range = 3/phi (correlation 0.05);
    • sfMsPGOcc fixes factor variances at 1 and loadings lower-triangular with unit diagonal, and sim.R matches;
    • msPGOcc gives species coefficients N(mu, tau^2) with tau^2 ~ IG(0.1, 0.1), and the module reports sqrt(tau^2).
  • Shared code. simulate() and fit() share only d_or() and the term-naming convention. My independent simulator shares nothing.

Interpretation of the small-design failure. Same generator, parameters and seeds, but with 4x the sites (480 vs 120):

  • psi_beta_elev bias went from -24% to -2.8%;
  • psi_beta_shrub went from +28% to +3.4%;
  • occupancy_prob went from +10.5% to +3.8%.

So the 120-site failure is small-sample behaviour of the posterior mean (logit slopes pulled away from zero with about 40 occupied sites, p = 0.3 and a spatial random effect), not a module or io bug. The builder's single_hard design (100 sites, p 0.25) shows the same pattern.

Coverage of the slopes stayed at 0.95-0.97 throughout, so the intervals remain honest; only the point estimates lean high. This is recorded as a design limit in README Pitfalls and in the report's "Validated limits" sentence. Users must run recover at their own design.

Engine cross-check on non-spatial data (spec item; builder's job had timed out)

dev/occu-spatial/verify/xc_nonspatial_v.R uses 64 datasets from the default design with sigma^2 = 0. It fits spPGOcc, PGOcc and unmarked::occu to each dataset (results/occu-spatial/verify/xc_nonspatial_engines.csv; log logs/occu-spatial_verify_xc_nonspatial.log).

PGOcc vs unmarked (independent likelihood engine):

  • per-dataset correlation >= 0.999 for all four parameters;
  • mean |difference|: 0.002 detection_prob, 0.006 occupancy_prob, 0.002 p_beta_effort, 0.026 psi_beta_forest (the posterior mean is slightly larger than the MLE, as expected).

spPGOcc vs PGOcc on the same non-spatial data:

  • detection agrees (mean |difference| 0.002-0.003);
  • psi_beta_forest is inflated: relative bias +20.8% for spPGOcc vs +8.2% for PGOcc and +5.0% for unmarked; mean |sp - ns| = 0.10; coverage 0.92 vs 0.94.

The IG(2,1) prior keeps sigma^2 away from 0 (median posterior mean 0.66), so the slopes become conditional on a spurious spatial effect. Limit: when the spatial variance posterior merely restates its prior, also fit PGOcc and report both. The report now says this in that case.

Usability (messy copies of the fisher example; dev/occu-spatial/verify/messy.R)

Before the fixes, these problems were handled silently or with unclear messages:

  • NA detection values were silently treated as surveys not done;
  • duplicated site x survey rows were silently accepted;
  • lat/long coordinates were silently accepted (every distance would be ~0 km);
  • a misspelled covariate column gave object 'elevation_m' not found.

All of these now give actionable messages. Missing site or survey covariates, a misspelled site id, non-0/1 values and duplicate coordinates were already caught. The site-id error now suggests the closest match ("'CAM-14' (did you mean 'CAM-014'?)").

Report check (fisher example): every number in the interpretation was recomputed from the estimates table. Examples:

  • OR per SD exp(1.010) = 2.75;
  • elevation per 200 m exp(-0.551 x 200/246) = 0.64;
  • detection per day exp(0.397/1.01) = 1.48;
  • the IG(2,1) prior 95% interval is 0.18-4.13.

The report uses association language, keeps detection and habitat effects apart, and gives effects on the probability scale. The conditional-effects curves (occupancy vs canopy; detection vs days active) were inspected: they pass through the reported values at the covariate means and include rug and 50/95% bands.

Changes made by the verifier

  • R/io.R:

    • missing-column errors list the file's actual columns and the config key to change;
    • formula terms are checked against the file;
    • empty detections are dropped with a note;
    • duplicate site x survey rows and duplicate site ids are flagged;
    • degree-like coordinates are flagged;
    • a misspelled site id gets a closest-match suggestion;
    • missing survey covariates report a count and example sites.
  • R/fit.R: data notes are passed into the diagnostics. With converge_on = "all", a note is added when R-hat >= 1.01 or bulk ESS < 400 (the real-data standard).

  • R/report.R:

    • the per-unit odds ratio now uses exp(posterior-mean slope), consistent with the per-SD odds ratio (previously the mean of exp(), e.g. 2.26 vs 2.19);
    • the spatial paragraph now compares the spatial-variance interval with its prior interval and fixes the awkward "8.8 (95% CrI ...) km" wording;
    • trace panels have readable labels;
    • the "Validated limits" sentence now states the measured limits instead of claiming unbiased recovery at all designs.
  • Estimation code is unchanged. Originals are kept in dev/occu-spatial/verify/*.R.orig.
  • R/report.R (later): when the spatial-variance interval matches its prior, the report now recommends also fitting PGOcc (see the engine cross-check above).
  • Note: these edits were made while the builder's xc_nimble.R spatial job (chinook, started 23:07) was still running. That R process had already loaded the module and only uses fit(), which is unchanged, so its results are unaffected.
