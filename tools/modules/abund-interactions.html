Occupancy

Abundance-mediated species interactions (dominant abundance -> subordinate occupancy)

Is the probability that a subordinate species (red squirrel) occupies a site associated with the number of individuals of a dominant species (pine marten) using that site, and does the strength or even the sign of that association depend on habitat (native broadleaf vs conifer plantation)? It also estimates the dominant species' site abundance and its habitat drivers.

module id: abund-interactions! Example verdict: OPEN11 of 12 quantities certified

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Camera-trap (or other repeated detection/non-detection) surveys of two species where one (dominant, e.g. a predator or competitor) may affect where the other (subordinate) occurs, and the question is whether the subordinate's occupancy is associated with HOW MANY dominant individuals use a site, possibly differently in different habitats.

Not for

Interactions that act through presence/absence only and symmetric co-occurrence (use occu-rota or occu-waddle); fewer than ~300 sites (a habitat-dependent interaction passed the gate at 600 sites in the default design but its size was overstated by ~12% at 700 sites in an independent design; Twining et al. 2025 recommend ~1000 sites; see VALIDATION.md); very rarely detected dominant species (per-individual daily detection well below 0.03, untested); counts of individually identified animals (use scr); temporal avoidance at the scale of hours (activity-overlap analyses).

The data you need

Camera deployments (site, start, end) + detection records (site, date, species), or a summarised site x occasion table of detection days and camera-days; site covariates (habitat), optional coordinates.

Try it

The worked example

Abundance-mediated species interactions: pine marten abundance and red squirrel occupancy (simulated camera-trap example)

The question: Is red squirrel occupancy lower at sites used by more pine martens, and does that association depend on the share of native broadleaf woodland?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have camera-trap data from 260 woodland sites in Northern Ireland: deployments.csv (camera start and end dates), records.csv (every photo, with species) and sites.csv (woodland cover and broadleaf share within 1 km). Is red squirrel occupancy lower where there are more pine martens, and does that depend on how much of the woodland is native broadleaf? Please give me a report with figures I can use in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Each 1-SD increase in woodland_pct (1 SD = 17.4 units) is associated with 1.70 (95% CI 1.47 to 1.96) times the expected Pine marten abundance (a +70% change) (per 10 units: rate ratio 1.36 (95% CI 1.25 to 1.47)).
  • Interaction at average habitat: each additional Pine marten individual at a site is associated with 0.59 (95% CI 0.39 to 0.85) times the odds that Red squirrel occupies the site (odds ratio per individual); on the logit scale the coefficient is -0.53 (95% CI -0.93 to -0.17) per individual. The posterior probability that the association is negative is > 0.99.
  • Predicted Red squirrel occupancy at average habitat: 0.62 (0.52-0.72) at a site with no Pine marten, 0.50 (0.42-0.57) with 1, 0.37 (0.25-0.49) with 2 and 0.26 (0.12-0.44) with 3 individuals (posterior medians, 95% CI).
  • Habitat dependence: the interaction changes by 0.69 (95% CI 0.30 to 1.14) logit units per individual for each 1-SD (20.8 units) increase in broadleaf_pct; the 95% interval excludes zero, so the data support an interaction whose strength depends on habitat.
  • Where broadleaf_pct = 12.9 (10th percentile): odds ratio per Pine marten individual 0.25 (95% CI 0.12 to 0.50) (negative).
  • Where broadleaf_pct = 65.2 (90th percentile): odds ratio per Pine marten individual 1.42 (95% CI 0.84 to 2.53) (not clearly different from zero).
  • Comparison with the common two-step shortcut (estimate Pine marten abundance first, then use it as a fixed covariate): the shortcut gives -0.48 (95% CI -0.81 to -0.15), with an interval 86% as wide as the joint model's. The joint model carries the uncertainty in each site's abundance into the interaction; the shortcut treats estimated abundance as if it were known, and in simulations it is biased towards zero with too-narrow intervals.
  • Caution: this is an association between the dominant's estimated abundance and the subordinate's occupancy, not proof of predation or competition. Unmodelled habitat preferences shared (or opposed) by the two species produce the same signal, so include the main habitat drivers of both species. Royle-Nichols abundance assumes individuals are detected independently and abundance is Poisson among sites; absolute abundance is less reliable than relative differences. Simulation checks for this module (VALIDATION.md) recovered the interaction without bias at 600 sites (4 weekly occasions of daily records), including when the dominant's per-individual daily detection was as low as 0.03; in an independent check with 700 sites and a weaker interaction its size was overstated by about 12%, and with 150-300 sites by roughly 10-20% (credible intervals kept near-nominal coverage). With fewer than about 1000 sites, read the interval rather than the point estimate.

Limits that matter

  • 260 sites is below the ~600 sites validated for a habitat-dependent interaction; at this design gamma0 is overstated by ~11% (coverage stays 0.95). Read intervals, not point estimates.
  • The broadleaf main effect on red squirrel occupancy is weakly identified at this design (planted-truth power 0.34).
  • Association, not causation: unmodelled habitat shared by both species loads onto the interaction term.
  • Royle-Nichols assumptions (Poisson abundance, independent detection of individuals, closure over 4 weeks) were not stress-tested; group-living or overdispersed martens would bias abundance and the interaction. The posterior predictive check is the only guard.
  • Abundance is the number of martens using a camera site, not a density; one marten can use several sites.
  • The two-step plug-in shortcut fails the gate (attenuated, overconfident) and is shown for comparison only.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  1. Fewer than ~600 sites with a habitat-varying interaction. At 300 sites gamma1 is biased +19% (Nimble) / +11% (ML on the same data), and the gate fails. At 150 sites the biases are -18% (gamma0) and +23% (gamma1). Interval coverage stays near nominal, so CrIs can still be read. The point estimate overstates the strength of the interaction. A constant interaction (no habitat term) is better determined, but it was not separately gate-tested below 600 sites.
  2. Dominant per-individual detection at the default (r = 0.10) at 300 sites: coverage of r was 0.86 (bound 0.885). At 600 sites it is 0.93.
  3. Low dominant detection (r = 0.03/day) passes at 600 sites. Lower values and fewer sites were not tested. Expect interactions to be weakly identified there, and look at the width of the CrI.
  4. Royle-Nichols assumptions (Poisson N, independent and identical detection of individuals, closure) were not stress-tested. Overdispersed or group-living dominants will bias N_i and so gamma. The posterior predictive check is the only guard, and its power was not measured.
  5. Direction and causality: the model assumes A affects S's occupancy and that S's detection does not depend on N_A. Shared unmodelled habitat drivers load onto gamma. Reports state "associated with", not "caused by".
  6. Two-step engine fails the gate (attenuated, overconfident). It is shown for comparison only.
  7. Run time: about 6 min per Nimble fit at 600 sites with fast settings (1 core per replicate); the full default settings took 7.7 min at 260 sites (4 cores, incl. GOF and two-step). Scale roughly linearly in sites.
  8. Not implemented: abundance (N-mixture) model for the subordinate; time-varying (dynamic) interactions; more than two species; spatially explicit N (SCR) for the dominant; covariates on the subordinate's detection that involve N_A.

How this module was validated

  • 100 simulated data sets per design fitted with Nimble (3 chains x 5000 iterations, 1500 burn-in) and with an exact ML engine on the same data; all 12 quantities passed on both engines at 600 sites.
  • A harder low-detection design (weekly detection about 0.19 for one marten) passed all 12; a smaller 300-site design failed on the habitat-by-interaction term (+19.4%).
  • Negative controls: a presence-only interaction model fitted to abundance-driven data failed (interaction -49%, coverage 0.64); the common two-step shortcut was biased toward no interaction and overconfident. With no true interaction, false-positive rates were 0.08 and 0.03.
  • Cross-checks: the dominant-species part matched unmarked::occuRN, and the reduced model matched the occu-single module within 0.015 on every parameter.
  • Power for the habitat-dependent interaction: 0.70, 0.88 and 1.00 at 150, 300 and 600 sites, with magnitudes overstated by 13-23% below 600 sites.
  • A separate verifier with its own simulator passed a 1400-site design, found a narrow failure (-11.6%) at 700 sites, confirmed the controls, and reproduced the builder's tables to every digit.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

All recovery runs: bin/Rscript core/run.R recover abund-interactions --config dev/abund-interactions/<cfg>.yaml --nsim 100 --cores 16 --out results/abund-interactions/recover_<cfg> (configs in dev/abund-interactions/; tables printed by dev/abund-interactions/val_tables.R straight from the saved *_recovery.rds, so no number below was typed by hand). Runs were made 2026-10-18/19 with R 4.5.3, nimble 1.4.3 and unmarked (ecotools Rlib/), on njobvu with 16 workers.

Recovery uses fast_settings: Nimble 3 chains x 5000 iterations, 1500 burn-in, thin 2 (5250 draws), no GOF and no two-step side fit. Replicate r uses seed 100000 + r. The Nimble, ML and two-step runs of a design therefore fit identical data sets (paired comparisons below).

Gate (core recovery_gate, unchanged): |bias| <= max(10% of |truth|, 3 MC SE), or <= max(0.1 x empirical SD, 3 MC SE) when truth = 0; coverage of the 95% interval >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (0.885 at nsim 100); >= 90% of replicates converged. Nimble "converged" means max R-hat < 1.05 over all monitored quantities. The real-data standard printed in reports (R-hat < 1.01, bulk ESS >= 400) is checked separately on every fit.

Default design (module defaults): 600 camera sites; 4 occasions of 7 camera-days each, with 10% of occasions losing a random number of days. Dominant species: mean 1 individual per site at average woodland, log-slope on woodland 0.5, per-individual daily detection r = 0.10 (weekly ~0.52 for one individual). Subordinate: occupancy 0.6 where the dominant is absent, logit slopes woodland 0.5 and broadleaf 0.3, interaction gamma0 = -0.7 per dominant individual at average broadleaf share and gamma1 = +0.5 per SD of broadleaf share (the interaction weakens, then reverses, in broadleaf-rich woodland, as in Twining et al. 2022 Proc R Soc B), daily detection 0.15. Simulated with the model in README.md (Royle-Nichols with K camera-days per occasion), as in Twining et al. (2025) simulation I.

Honesty note on the default design: the first full runs used 300 sites as the default (folders results/abund-interactions/n300_recover_*). There gamma1 was biased +19% (Nimble) and +11% (ML on the same data), so the joint model failed the gate. The gate fails, as required. A presence-only interaction misstates the per-individual effect: the presence effect, -1.05, absorbs the effect of 1.6 individuals on average where martens are present. It also pulls the subordinate's woodland response towards zero, because woodland drives marten abundance. 4b. Joint model vs two-step plug-in: does the joint model propagate the uncertainty in N_A?

The twostep engine fits unmarked::occuRN to the dominant and takes the empirical-Bayes posterior mean E[N_i | y]. It then fits unmarked::occu to the subordinate with that as a fixed covariate. This is the common shortcut. It runs on the same 100 default data sets as section 1 (median 4 s/rep):

Paired on identical data (n = 100): the two-step interval is narrower in the median data set, by 0.869x the joint width for gamma0 and 0.858x for gamma1. The two-step point estimates correlate 0.92-0.94 with the joint ones but are shrunk towards zero. This is classical errors-in-variables attenuation: E[N|y] is a noisy, shrunk proxy for N. Ignoring that noise makes the shortcut both biased towards "no interaction" and overconfident. The joint model gets both right. At 300 sites the two-step gamma0 is again attenuated (+10.6%, coverage 0.87). Every report shows the two-step fit beside the joint fit (compare_twostep: TRUE, figure joint_vs_twostep) but never uses it for inference.

Reading: with gamma0 = -0.7 per individual, the constant part of the interaction is detected (power >= 0.92) even at 150 sites. The habitat-dependence (gamma1 = 0.5 per SD) needs about 300 sites for 80% power and 600 for 100%. Below 600 sites both interaction terms are biased away from zero, by 13-23%: magnitudes are overstated, an effect that falls as n grows (the usual small-sample inflation of logistic coefficients, amplified by the latent covariate). Coverage stays >= 0.94 throughout, so intervals remain honest even where the point estimate is biased. With nsim 50, power and coverage have MC SE of about 0.03-0.07 and bias about 3-5%.

Independent verification (2026-10-19, separate verifier agent)

Done by an agent that did not build the module. Scripts are in dev/abund-interactions/verify/, saved results in results/abund-interactions/verify/, and logs in logs/abund-interactions_verify*.log. The gate is core recovery_gate with no threshold changes.

Literature. The model was checked line by line against Twining, Augustine, Royle & Fuller (2025, Ecology 106:e4468; PMC11725697):

  • N_i^D ~ Poisson(lambda_i), with log(lambda_i) linear.
  • y^D_ij ~ Binomial(K, 1 - (1 - r_ij)^N_i).
  • z^S_i ~ Bernoulli(psi_i), with logit(psi_i) = beta X + gamma0 N_i + gamma1 N_i x cov_i.
  • y^S_ij ~ Bernoulli(p z_i). The module generalises this to Binomial(K_ij, z_i p_ij) over camera-days.

The Nimble code (abi_nimble_code), the exact-likelihood engine (abi_site_loglik) and the simulator all implement this model.

  • gamma0 is the slope at the standardised modifier = 0, which is average habitat.
  • The paper's sample-size findings match the README. A constant interaction was unbiased at 300 sites x 4 occasions. A covariate-varying interaction was biased below 600 sites x 10 single 0/1 occasions and unbiased at 1000 sites.
  • The paper's priors are "vague" and given only in its Appendix S1. The module's Logistic(0,1) and N(0, SD 2) priors are a reasonable weakly informative choice.

The simulator and fit share only the formula and standardisation conventions. The planted-truth check below uses a separately written simulator, so a shared bug would have shown up there.

  • The bias disappears at 1400 sites.
  • Coverage stays nominal.
  • The posterior median is no better than the mean (-10.6% vs -11.2% on the first 48 replicates; median_diag.R).

Changes made by the verifier (only R/io.R and R/report.R were changed; none affect the likelihood or the samplers, so the recovery results above stand):

  1. read_data:

    • fm$p partial-matched fm$psi, so leaving out p: silently gave detection the occupancy covariates. Fixed by using [[ ]].
    • Unknown keys under formulas: now stop with an error.
    • A missing formulas: section now gives a warning.
    • Site/species whitespace is trimmed.
    • Empty dates or site ids, duplicate covariate rows and site-id typos are caught.
  2. Factor habitat modifier: the report said gamma0 applied "at average habitat". It now says "where habitat = <reference level>", and each contrast is worded with its level and reference.
  3. The detection unit is now "camera-day" or "survey occasion" as appropriate. "Multiplies the odds" was reworded to "is associated with ... times the odds".

  4. Figures:

    • The coefficient forest-plot caption now says the slopes are per 1 SD (the axis labels carry raw units) and uses "credible" or "confidence" according to the engine.
    • The x-axis labels of the abundance map overlapped at 6-digit eastings. Fixed with 3 breaks and check.overlap.
  5. The SMALL DESIGN note and the report caution now cite the 700-site independent result. The README not_for and Simulation sections say the same.

Verdict: VERIFIED_WITH_LIMITS.

  • The module passes the gate at its default design (600 sites, builder) and at an independent 1400-site design.
  • The negative control fails, and the FPR is about 0.05.
  • At an independent 700-site design with a weaker interaction, the Nimble posterior mean overstates the interaction at average habitat by 11.6%, which is just outside the 10% rule. Coverage stays nominal.
  • For a habitat-varying interaction with fewer than about 1000 sites, read the credible interval rather than the point estimate, and run a planted-truth check at the user's own design.
