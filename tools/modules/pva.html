Demography and population dynamics

Population viability analysis - count-based (diffusion / state-space) and matrix-model PVA

"How likely is this population to drop to a level we would consider functionally extinct (the quasi-extinction threshold) within the next 10, 25 or 50 years; how fast is it growing or declining in the long run; and which vital rates, or which management actions, would most reduce the risk?"

module id: pva! Example verdict: OPEN6 of 7 quantities certified

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

The question is extinction or quasi-extinction RISK - "how likely is this population to fall below N animals within 10, 25 or 50 years, and which vital rates or management actions change that?" Use the count model when you have one count or index per year (10+ years, ideally 20+). Use the matrix model when you have stage- or age-specific vital rates (survival, fecundity) with standard errors and year-to-year variation, e.g. from a CJS, IPM or nest-monitoring analysis, or raw annual monitoring counts of animals at risk and survivors / young produced.

Not for

Estimating trend, density dependence or covariate effects on growth from counts (use timeseries, which this module complements); estimating survival from capture histories (cjs / multistate / ipm first, then feed their estimates here); spatially explicit or metapopulation PVA (not supported); fewer than ~10 counts (risk is unestimable); populations with strong density dependence near carrying capacity in the count model (see Pitfalls).

The data you need

Count model - CSV with one row per year (year, count; blank = not surveyed). matrix model - a matrix definition (stages, entries as formulas in named vital rates) in the config plus EITHER a vital-rate CSV (rate, type, mean, se, sd_env, n_years) OR an annual monitoring CSV (year, rate, n, events); initial abundance per stage; a quasi-extinction threshold..

The worked example

Shorebird breeding-pair survey: count-based state-space PVA with counting error and missing years (simulated)

The question: Is the shorebird population really declining, and how likely is it to fall to 40 breeding pairs or fewer within 10 and 25 years?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have 30 years of spring counts of breeding pairs for a shorebird (shorebird_breeding_pairs.csv; blank = no survey). The counts are surveys, so they're not exact. Please tell me whether the population is declining and how likely it is to drop to 40 breeding pairs or fewer within 10 and 25 years. I don't code, so please do the analysis and give me a report and figures I can use.

What the report says

Results from the example data

What this means

  • the population was not clearly increasing or decreasing: the long-run (median) growth rate is lambda = 0.942 per year (95% CI 0.879 to 1.013), i.e. a typical change of -5.8% per year (95% CI -12.1% to +1.3%).
  • Year-to-year variability in the log growth rate (process variance sigma^2) is 0.0188 (95% CI 0.0030 to 0.0971).
  • Counting (observation) error variance on the log scale is 0.0256 (95% CI 0.0003 to 0.0727), equivalent to a coefficient of variation of roughly 16% in a single count. The model's estimate of the true current abundance is 79 breeding pairs (95% CI 61 to 100).
  • Probability that the population falls to or below the quasi-extinction threshold of 40 breeding pairs in at least one annual census within 10 years: 49% (95% CI 6% to 92%); 25 years: 90% (95% CI 31% to >99.9%).
  • Forecasts beyond about 20% of the monitoring span (here 6 years) are known to be very imprecise (Fieberg & Ellner 2000); use them to compare options, not as absolute predictions.

Limits that matter

  • Quasi-extinction point estimates are median-unbiased but too extreme on average and fail the mean-bias gate; quote the interval (Known limit 1).
  • Risk horizons beyond ~20% of the data span (here ~6 yr) give intervals spanning most of 0-1 (Fieberg & Ellner 2000).
  • Low power: a true 3.9%/yr decline at this design is detected (CI for mu excludes 0) in only 22.5% of simulated data sets.
  • Counting-error variance can be biased low (-18%) when counting error exceeds process noise in short gappy series (here -4%).
  • Density dependence, autocorrelated environments, catastrophes and trends are not tested by recovery.
All known limits from the module's validation record

  1. Point estimates of quasi-extinction probability fail the mean-bias gate at every design.

    • They are median-unbiased and rank risk correctly, but they are too extreme: high risks are overstated and very low risks understated.
    • Their intervals are calibrated (coverage 0.895-1.00).
    • Treat risk as an interval and use it to compare scenarios; never quote the point as a forecast.
    • Beyond about 20% of the data span, intervals typically cover most of 0-1 (Fieberg & Ellner 2000; Ellner & Holmes 2008).
  2. Short series (12 years of counts): risk-interval coverage falls to 0.895-0.915, just under the 0.904 bound at 10-25 yr.
  3. Short matrix monitoring (8 years): under-coverage of adult survival (0.895), and the fecundity environmental SD is biased low (-16%).

  4. Stochastic lambda intervals in the matrix model are conservative.

    • Coverage is 1.00 at the default design (0.985 at the short one), and the false-positive rate is 0/200.
    • The SE inflation by t(0.975, years-1)/1.96 plus propagation of environmental-variance uncertainty makes them wider than necessary.
    • Power to detect lambda != 1 is therefore lower than nominal.
  5. Observation-error variance is biased low (-18%) when counting error exceeds process noise in short series with gaps.

  6. Assumptions not tested by recovery, because the simulator obeys the model:

    • density dependence in the count model (Sabo et al. 2004);
    • autocorrelated environments;
    • catastrophes and trends in vital rates;
    • imperfect detection in raw monitoring data (option B assumes detection = 1; use cjs first).
  7. Matrix recovery uses known-fate monitoring data (option B input). Option A (vital-rate table with SEs) is checked only through the popbio cross-checks and the example.
  8. The diffusion model treats the last count as the true current abundance (N_current has no uncertainty), as in Dennis et al. (1991). Use state_space when counts are surveys.

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated datasets per design. The true extinction risk was computed with an exact recursion, checked against 1 million simulated paths (within 4.8e-4). To pass, bias had to be within 10% (or 3 Monte Carlo SE) and coverage (how often the 95% interval contained the truth) at least 0.904.
  • Default designs (count diffusion, count state-space, stage matrix): growth rate, variances, current abundance, vital rates and elasticities passed. Quasi-extinction point estimates failed the mean-bias test, but their intervals covered the truth 0.945-1.00 of the time.
  • Harder designs: with 12 years of counts, risk-interval coverage was 0.895-0.900. A 15-year state-space series with counting error larger than process noise biased observation variance by -18%. Eight years of matrix data gave adult-survival coverage of 0.895.
  • Negative controls: fitting the diffusion model to data with counting error inflated process variance by 209% (coverage 0.015). Ignoring parameter uncertainty in the matrix model dropped coverage to 0.000-0.06.
  • False-positive rates for a decline with no true trend: 0.055 (diffusion) and 0.040 (state-space). The matrix model was conservative (0.000).
  • Cross-checks: estimates were identical to Morris and Doak's grizzly bear example and agreed with popbio, MARSS, and Nimble MCMC (median difference 0.03 posterior SD).
  • Power to detect a 3%-per-year decline was 0.060 with 10 years of counts and 0.335 with 60. A separate verifier agent tested three designs of its own, confirmed the risk point-estimate limit, and found 5-year risk coverage of 0.88.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Planted truth for risk. For each replicate, the true quasi-extinction probability is computed from the TRUE parameters, starting from the TRUE final abundance.

  • Count model: an exact numerical first-passage recursion for annual censuses (pva_qext_exact). It agrees with 1e6-path brute force to within 4.8e-4 (at most 2.6 MC SE) on a 24-cell grid of (mu, sigma2, d) (logs/pva_crosscheck_exact.log).

1. Literature / equation check

Checked line by line against Dennis et al. (1991) and Morris & Doak (2002, ch. 3 and 8), and against the popbio source code (countCDFxt, extCDF, stoch.growth.rate, betaval, stretchbetaval):

  • mu-hat = sum(log ratios)/sum(intervals); sigma2-hat from the regression through the origin with q-1 df; t interval for mu, chi-square interval for sigma2.
  • Inverse-Gaussian first-passage CDF; BGK continuity constant 0.5826 = -zeta(1/2)/sqrt(2 pi).
  • Beta / stretched-beta / lognormal moment parameterisations, the same as popbio.
  • Tuljapurkar: log lambda_s ~ log lambda1 - S'CS/(2 lambda1^2), the same as popbio::stoch.growth.rate.
  • Kendall (1998) moment estimator for the environmental variance.

All agree. Bootstrap difference: popbio's countCDFxt truncates the bootstrap draws of mu and sigma2 to their 95% CIs; this module does not, so its risk intervals are wider and not truncated, which is the correct propagation.

Simulator/fit sharing:

  • count_simulate is independent of the fit. Its truth uses pva_qext_exact, not the fitted formula.
  • matrix_simulate and the matrix truth reuse mx_vr_year / mx_project from the fit, so a projection bug could cancel out.

For that reason the matrix truth here was recomputed with fully separate code (below).

4. Usability (messy data; dev/pva/verify2/messy_test.R, logs logs/pva_verify2_messy*.log)

Problems found and fixed:

  1. Absolute data paths in a config failed. They were pasted onto the config folder ("Count file not found: .../messy//nfs4/..."). New pva_path() in R/count.R resolves absolute paths as given; it is used for the count file, vital-rate table, monitoring file and correlation CSV.
  2. A blank n/events cell in the monitoring CSV crashed check_data with "missing value where TRUE/FALSE needed". Rates are now estimated from the valid rows, the checks are NA-safe, and the message names the bad row ("Missing, zero or negative n / events in row(s) 3 ... (2009 s_ad)").
  3. initial_abundance with a misspelled stage name (e.g. adults) was used positionally without any warning, so a typo in the names went unnoticed. It now stops with the names that don't match.
  4. settings: model typo now says "must be 'count' or 'matrix' (got 'matrx')". The misspelled-rate message ends in a question.

Messages that were already good: non-numeric count typo (names row, year and value), wrong column name (lists the columns present), duplicated year, zero count, threshold above the last count, missing threshold, misspelled rate id (suggests the nearest name), wrong monitoring column, rate type typo, survival mean outside (0,1).

5. Reports and figures (all 4 examples refitted; dev/pva/verify2/ex2/, results/pva/example*/)

Every interpretation sentence was checked against the estimates table, including growth rates and percentages, sigma-to-% conversion, the CV of counting error, risks and their CIs, elasticities and scenario differences.

Fixed:

  • (a) The state-space methods text said "point estimates are posterior medians". The sigma2 and tau2 point estimates are REML values. The text now says so.
  • (b) An upper limit of 0.996 printed as "100%"; it now prints "99.6%".
  • (c) With monitoring-data input, the text said SEs were "supplied". It now says "estimated from the monitoring data".
  • (d) The normality and lag-1 checks used increments divided by the interval length s. With missing years these increments are not identically distributed, so they are now standardised by sqrt(s). The shorebird non-normality flag persists (p = 0.003), so it reflects the data.

No causal language was found. Scenario sentences say "projection ... not evidence that the action will achieve it".

6. Limits added by this verification
  • Diffusion risk at short horizons (5 yr) has coverage 0.88-0.89 under the gate. This is driven by misses at true risks < 0.3%, plus a few overestimated near-threshold cases.

  • With vital-rate-table input:

    • the module trusts the supplied sd_env; small environmental SDs estimated from short series are biased low (-15% to -18% here);
    • z-type SEs give vital-rate coverage of only 0.91-0.93.
  • State-space intervals for mu are conservative (coverage 0.985-0.99; false-positive rate 0.015).

Engine, references and full details

Engine: hand-written R - Dennis et al. diffusion estimators and inverse-Gaussian first-passage risk with a discrete-census correction; Bayesian exponential-growth state-space (EGSS) model computed exactly on a grid over the REML likelihood (no MCMC); vectorised stochastic matrix projection with demographic and environmental stochasticity and parameter uncertainty. popbio, MARSS and a Nimble MCMC of the same state-space model used as independent cross-checks..
