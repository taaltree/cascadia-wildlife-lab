Distribution and community

Community composition - ordination, RDA and variation partitioning, PERMANOVA with dispersion test, beta-diversity partitioning, indicator species

A site x species table (counts, cover, biomass or presence/absence) from many sites plus a site table of environmental variables and/or groups (habitat types, treatments, regions, years); questions like "how much of the variation in species composition is related to elevation vs canopy cover?", "do burned and unburned plots hold different communities?", "is beta diversity driven by species replacement or species loss?", "which species characterise each habitat?", or "draw me an ordination".

module id: community! Example verdict: OPENestimates accurate · intervals too narrow

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

A site x species table (counts, cover, biomass or presence/absence) from many sites plus a site table of environmental variables and/or groups (habitat types, treatments, regions, years); questions like "how much of the variation in species composition is related to elevation vs canopy cover?", "do burned and unburned plots hold different communities?", "is beta diversity driven by species replacement or species loss?", "which species characterise each habitat?", or "draw me an ordination".

Not for

Individual species' responses with uncertainty from imperfect detection (use occu-single / occu-spatial / nmixture, or a joint species distribution model, jsdm); species interactions (occu-rota, occu-waddle); fewer than about 10 sites or 3 species; time series of one community (timeseries); phylogenetic or functional beta diversity (betapart can do it, not wired here); causal claims about what drives composition (use causal / sem with stated assumptions).

The data you need

Species CSV, wide (one row per site, one column per species) or long (site, species, count); site CSV with the same site ids, numeric environmental variables and an optional grouping column.

Try it

The worked example

Forest bird communities along elevation and canopy gradients and among stand types (simulated Oregon Cascades point counts)

The question: How much of the variation in breeding-bird community composition among 60 stations goes with elevation versus canopy cover; do old growth, thinned and young plantation stands hold different communities (and differ in variability); is beta diversity turnover or nestedness; and which species indicate each stand type?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have bird point counts from 60 forest stations (cascades_bird_counts.csv, one column per species) and a station table with stand type, elevation and canopy cover (cascades_stations.csv). How much of the variation in the bird community goes with elevation versus canopy cover, do old growth, thinned and young plantation stands hold different communities, is it turnover or nestedness, and which species are indicators of each stand type? Please give me a report and figures I can use in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Together the variables accounted for 29.3% (95% CI 24.6% to 34.0%) of the among-site variation (adjusted R2, p = 0.001), leaving 70.7% unexplained.
  • Elevation (m) alone 24.6% (95% CI 20.0% to 29.1%) (p = 0.001); elevation after accounting for the other variables 21.7% (95% CI 15.7% to 27.6%) (p = 0.001); Canopy cover (%) alone 7.7% (95% CI 2.7% to 12.6%) (p = 0.001); canopy cover after accounting for the other variables 4.8% (95% CI 2.4% to 7.2%) (p = 0.001).
  • The shared fraction, 2.9% (95% CI -1.9% to 7.8%), is variation that cannot be attributed to one variable because the variables are correlated with each other; it is not an interaction and has no test. [Agent note: its interval failed the planted-truth coverage gate at this design (0.890); report it as descriptive.]
  • The association is correlational: unmeasured variables that co-vary with elevation (m) could produce the same pattern.
  • Stand type groups differed in composition: group membership accounted for 3.9% (95% CI 0.9% to 6.9%) of the variation in Bray-Curtis dissimilarities (PERMANOVA adjusted R2, p = 0.011).
  • Because PERMDISP found no dispersion difference (p = 0.776), the PERMANOVA result is most consistent with a shift in average species composition (location). PERMDISP has limited power for modest differences in spread, though, so a smaller contribution from dispersion cannot be ruled out.
  • Golden-crowned Kinglet was an indicator of young plantation after false-discovery-rate correction (IndVal 0.78, A 0.83, B 0.72, p = 0.030).
  • Two sites differed on average by Sorensen dissimilarity 0.63 (95% CI 0.61 to 0.66); 85% (95% CI 81% to 88%) of this was turnover. Composition changes mainly by replacement.

Limits that matter

  • Shared fraction: jackknife CI too narrow at this design (coverage 0.890 over 200 replicates) and when groups differ in variability or n < ~30 sites; report it without an interval; it has no test (VALIDATION.md limits 1-3).
  • Jackknife CIs are not tests: use permutation p-values to decide whether an effect exists; the PERMANOVA R2 CI is conservative (limit 4).
  • PERMANOVA confounds location and dispersion in unbalanced designs; here groups are near-balanced (22/20/18) and PERMDISP p = 0.78, but PERMDISP has limited power (it missed the simulated 1.4x dispersion of young plantations) and is mildly liberal (7.2% at alpha 0.05) (limit 5).
  • No detection or effort model: results describe birds as counted on one 10-min visit; missed birds inflate apparent turnover (limit 6).
  • Sites assumed exchangeable; no restricted permutations for clustered stations (limit 7).
  • Associations, not causes: elevation stands in for correlated factors.
  • NMDS, DCA, CCA, db-RDA, multi-site beta and indicator values are descriptive, checked by tests and inspection only (limit 8); NMDS did not repeat its best solution in 50 starts (stress 0.163).
All known limits from the module's validation record

  1. Small designs (about 24 sites or fewer).

    • The jackknife intervals for the shared fraction (coverage 0.855) and for mean nestedness (0.885) are too narrow.
    • The unique fraction of a weak gradient is biased upward by about 10%.
    • With fewer than about 30 sites, report the shared fraction and nestedness as descriptive values without interval claims.

  2. Strongly heteroscedastic sites (one group 3× noisier; Section 4d).

    • Jackknife coverage drops to 0.84–0.86 for the marginal R2 of the weaker gradient and for the shared fraction.
    • The PERMANOVA R2 is biased in whichever direction the group-size and dispersion imbalance pushes it.
    • When PERMDISP is significant, treat these intervals and the PERMANOVA R2 with caution.
  3. The shared fraction at a true value of 0 has jackknife coverage of 0.83, and the shared fraction has no test (Peres-Neto et al. 2006).

  4. Jackknife CIs are not tests.

    • The marginal R2adj CI excludes 0 in 8.5% of null datasets.
    • The PERMANOVA R2 CI almost never excludes 0 under weak effects.
    • Use the permutation p-values for hypothesis tests and the CIs for effect sizes.
  5. PERMANOVA confounds location and dispersion in unbalanced designs (validated in Section 4c). This is a property of the method, so the module reports PERMDISP alongside it.
  6. No detection or effort model. Truths and estimates are for the community as sampled. Unequal effort among sites shows up as composition differences and nestedness.
  7. No restricted permutations for nested or blocked designs (plots within stands). Sites are assumed to be exchangeable.
  8. Descriptive outputs have no recovery test: NMDS, DCA, CCA, db-RDA, multi-site β, the dispersion ratio and indicator values. They are checked by the tests in Section 4c and by inspection only.

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated communities at the default design (no MCMC; delete-one-site jackknife intervals), with truths computed from large independent Monte Carlo samples; all 11 estimands PASSED (bias at most 3%, coverage 0.910-0.975).
  • Other designs: presence/absence analysis and a strong-gradient design with 90 sites passed all 11; a small hard design (24 sites, half effort) failed 3 of 11 (shared fraction coverage 0.855, nestedness 0.885, a weak unique fraction +10% bias).
  • Negative controls: reporting unadjusted R2 failed 4 of 11 (up to +249% bias); groups that differ only in dispersion in an unbalanced design failed on the PERMANOVA effect size (+36%).
  • False-positive rates under no effect: PERMANOVA 3.7-5.2%, PERMDISP 4.7-6.8%, indicator species 1.0% in 200 datasets per scenario; a larger 1000-dataset study found PERMDISP mildly liberal (7.2%).
  • Cross-checks against vegan, ade4 and betapart matched to within 4.4e-16.
  • Power over 15-60 sites: a strong gradient is detected with 15 sites; a moderate one needs about 36 sites for 90% power.
  • A separate verifier agent built a structurally different simulator (negative binomial counts, 40 sites, 22 species, 4 unbalanced regions) that passed 11/11, reproduced the default run line for line, and improved data messages and report wording; verdict: verified with limits.

What "truth" means here (read first)

This module is descriptive and multivariate. Its estimands are population values of statistics, not parameters of a likelihood. Each truth in simulate() is the value the statistic converges to for an infinite set of sites drawn from the same process: the same species pool, the same distribution of environmental gradients, and groups in the design's proportions. It is computed by large Monte Carlo samples that are independent of the analysed dataset (seed + 7,777,777). See simulate.R.

All truths describe the community as sampled (Poisson counts at the given effort, no detection model). An undersampled survey has more apparent turnover than the latent community, and the truth reflects that by construction.

Each replicate draws a new species pool, so emp_sd in the summaries includes variation in the truth between replicates. Bias and coverage are computed per replicate against that replicate's truth.

Uncertainty: delete-one-site jackknife SE with t_{n−1} 95% intervals for every estimand that has a truth.

Simulator and default design

  • Species: 30 species with Gaussian niches on two gradients.

    • Tolerances: 0.2 (elevation) and 0.4 (soil moisture) of the sampled range, each × logN(0, 0.3²).
    • Peak abundance: logN(log 4, 0.8²).
  • Groups: 3 habitat groups with species-specific location effects δ ~ N(0, 0.5²) on log abundance.
  • Noise: mean-preserving site × species lognormal noise, SD 0.5.
  • Design: 60 sites, gradient correlation 0.4, Poisson counts.
  • Recovery uses fast_settings: estimands only, with no permutation tests or ordinations. One replicate takes 25–110 s, mostly the truth Monte Carlo.

The ×3 rows come from dev/community/permanova_rates_disp3.R and results/community/validation/permanova_rates_disp3/. The mean PERMANOVA R2adj was:

  • 0.0160 against a truth of 0.0113 (small group more variable): inflated by 42%.
  • 0.0056 against a truth of 0.0090 (large group more variable): deflated by 38%.
  • 0.0158 against a truth of 0.0157 (balanced): unbiased.

This is the direction Anderson & Walsh (2013) describe: PERMANOVA is liberal when the smaller group is more dispersed and conservative when the larger group is.

Findings:

  • Permutation tests hold their level under the null.

    • PERMANOVA rejects 3.7% (2 groups) and 5.2% (3 groups).
    • PERMDISP rejects 4.7% and 6.8%.
    • Indicator species (any species significant after BH correction) rejects 1.0%.

  • Dispersion-only differences produce PERMANOVA "differences in composition".

    • Balanced design: the rate stays near nominal (5.2%).
    • Unbalanced, with the small group the more variable one: PERMANOVA rejects 9.3% at ×2 noise. At ×3 noise the R2 estimate is inflated by 42% when the small group is the more variable one, and deflated by 38% in the mirror design.
    • PERMDISP flags the dispersion difference in 40–79% of datasets at ×2 and in 92–100% at ×3. This is why the module always reports PERMDISP next to PERMANOVA, and why interpret() warns when both are significant, naming the unbalanced case (Anderson & Walsh 2013).
  • Simulator caveat (honest). In this simulator, "dispersion only" means extra mean-preserving lognormal noise on expected counts. That adds zeros, so the Bray-Curtis population centroid also moves slightly: truth PERMANOVA R2 is 0.002–0.003 at ×2 and 0.009–0.016 at ×3, not 0. With differing noise, the zero-inflation changes the expected Bray-Curtis profile itself. Part of the excess PERMANOVA rejection is therefore a real but tiny location difference. The ×2 balanced scenario (5.2% rejection) shows the shift is too small to detect on its own.
  • The jackknife CI for PERMANOVA R2 is very conservative for detection. In the location scenarios it never excluded 0, while the permutation test rejected 13–17%. Its coverage is still 0.95–0.98. Use the PERMANOVA p-value for testing and the jackknife CI as an interval for the effect size.

7. Power

Power is the share of 95% jackknife CIs excluding 0, at default effect sizes.

What this means for design:

  • A strong gradient (about 30% of variation) is detected with 15 sites.
  • A moderate one (about 10%) needs about 36 sites for 90% power.
  • A weak group effect (truth R2 about 0.04) is rarely excluded from 0 by the conservative jackknife CI. Plan group designs with the PERMANOVA permutation test instead (Section 4c).

The lowest coverages (0.85–0.88) belong to R2adj_shared and beta_sne, consistent with Section 3. results/community/validation/power/

Independent verification (2026-10-20, separate verifier agent)

The verifier wrote its own simulator and truth code in dev/community/verify/verify_indep.R. Only module$fit() is used from the module; the gate is core's summarise_recovery + recovery_gate.

The verifier's simulator differs from the builder's in every structural choice:

  • Counts are negative binomial (size 1.5), not Poisson-lognormal.
  • Temperature is Normal (12 + 4·z, °C). The niche is Gaussian in temperature only.
  • Canopy (%) = 100·Φ(z2), and species respond log-linearly to canopy, not unimodally.
  • Gradient correlation is 0.5.
  • 40 sites in 4 unbalanced regions (13/9/9/9), 22 species, region effect SD 0.6.

Truth definitions:

  • R2 truths: population R2 of the Hellinger matrix on the reported variables, from 50,000 sites.
  • PERMANOVA truth: 1 − Σ π_g E[d²|g,g] / E[d²|mixture], from 40,000 pairs per term.
  • β truths: mean over 80,000 random pairs of distinct design sites.

Literature and implementation checks:

  • Baselga (2010) components agree with betapart::beta.pair source: β_sne = ((max−min)/(2a+b+c))·(a/(a+min)) = β_sor − β_sim.
  • Adjusted R2 (Ezekiel) and the varpart fraction algebra follow Peres-Neto et al. (2006).
  • PERMANOVA SS_T = (1/N)Σd², SS_W = Σ_g (1/n_g)Σ_g d² (Anderson 2001).
  • dca_axis1_length_sd equals vegan's printed DCA axis length (4.6377 on the example).
  • dev/community/verify/xcheck_indep.R compares the module with vegan::varpart, adonis2 and betapart::beta.pair on 3 datasets from the verifier's simulator. Maximum absolute difference: 3.3e-16.

Usability:

  • fit on the example config: all 11 truths lie inside their 95% CIs.
  • Every interpretation sentence was checked against the estimates. Correlational wording is used, and the as-sampled (no detection model) caveat is present.
  • The five required figures (ordination, variance_partition, beta_components, dispersion, indicator_species) plus six optional ones were inspected. Plotted values equal fit$estimates, and axes are in original units.

  • Messy data (dev/community/verify/messy/) gives clear, actionable messages:

Additional limits found:

  • PERMDISP is mildly liberal (7.2% ± 0.8% at α = 0.05 with groups of about 13 sites).
  • With presence/absence data, the PERMANOVA R2 point estimate runs about 25% high at 40 sites. Its CI is conservative (coverage 0.98).
  • The shared fraction runs about 9% low at 40 sites when it is small.

Verdict: VERIFIED_WITH_LIMITS. The default design passes. The documented limits (small designs, heteroscedasticity, shared fraction at 0, PERMDISP slightly liberal) remain.

Engine, references and full details

Engine: hand-written RDA / variation partitioning (adjusted R2), PERMANOVA sums of squares and Baselga (2010) pairwise components with delete-one-site jackknife intervals (identical to vegan::varpart, RsquareAdj, adonis2 and betapart::beta.pair); vegan permutation tests, NMDS, DCA, CCA, db-RDA, envfit, betadisper; betapart (multi-site); indicspecies (IndVal.g).
