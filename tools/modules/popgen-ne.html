Conservation genetics

Effective population size and relatedness

Genotypes (microsatellites or SNPs) from one or more samples of a population; question is how large the genetically effective population is (Ne), how fast it is losing diversity, whether Ne is above conservation thresholds (50/100, 500/1000), and which sampled individuals are close relatives (full sibs, half sibs, parent-offspring).

module id: popgen-ne! Example verdict: OPEN3 of 5 quantities certified

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Genotypes (microsatellites or SNPs) from one or more samples of a population; question is how large the genetically effective population is (Ne), how fast it is losing diversity, whether Ne is above conservation thresholds (50/100, 500/1000), and which sampled individuals are close relatives (full sibs, half sibs, parent-offspring).

Not for

Population structure, FST, isolation by distance, assignment (use popgen-structure); demographic history over many generations (coalescent methods such as GONE/SNeP); census abundance (use capture-recapture or close-kin mark-recapture models); pedigree reconstruction with parentage likelihoods (COLONY, sequoia).

The data you need

One row per individual with population, optional sampling time and optional flag to exclude juveniles/siblings from Ne; diploid genotypes as two allele columns per locus, "152/160" single columns, SNP 0/1/2 dosages, GENEPOP or VCF; optional list of known relative pairs; optional census size and generation time for the temporal method.

Try it

The worked example

Effective population size and relatedness of a bull trout spawning population from two microsatellite samples (simulated data with known truth)

Bull trout (Salvelinus confluentus) are listed as threatened in the United States. They are cold-water char that spawn in small headwater streams. A biologist monitors one spawning population, Upper Elk Creek. Redd counts suggest about 120 spawning adults.

The question: How large is the genetically effective population (Ne) of the Upper Elk Creek bull trout, how fast is it losing heterozygosity, and can 16 microsatellites identify close relatives?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have microsatellite genotypes for bull trout from Upper Elk Creek in bulltrout_microsats.csv: 50 adults sampled in 2014 and 50 in 2024, 16 loci, two columns per locus, 0 = missing. There are also 12 juveniles from 2024, two from each of six redds; known_relationships.csv lists the nest-mate pairs. Generation time is about 5 years and redd counts suggest about 120 adults. Please estimate the effective population size and how fast the population is losing genetic diversity, and tell me whether the markers can identify relatives. I'd like a report with figures for a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

What this means

  • Temporal method (2014 and 2024, 2.0 generations apart, sampling plan I, census N = 120): Jorde & Ryman's Fs' = 0.0082 gives Ne = 122 (95% CI 55.4 to 407). Nei-Tajima and Pollak estimators give 127 and 124.
  • Sample 2014 (50 individuals, 16 loci): the linkage-disequilibrium estimate of Ne is 121 (95% CI 74.4 to 271). An Ne of this size loses about 0.41% of its heterozygosity per generation, about 4.1% over 10 generations, unless immigration or mutation replaces it.
  • Sample 2024: the linkage-disequilibrium estimate of Ne is 139 (95% CI 76.0 to 500).
  • The interval lies above 50 but reaches below 100: the population passes the classic short-term threshold (50) but not clearly the revised one (100; Frankham et al. 2014).
  • Relatedness in sample 2024 (1891 pairs): 15 pairs have r >= 0.354 (likely first-degree) and 86 have 0.177 <= r < 0.354 (likely second-degree); about 7.0% of truly unrelated pairs would be called second-degree or closer; 80% of full sibs and 46% of half sibs fall in their own class.
  • Known fullsib pairs (n = 6) have mean r = 0.494 (95% CI 0.296 to 0.693), against a pedigree expectation of 0.50.
  • F_IS = 0.028 (95% CI -0.026 to 0.081) in 2014 and 0.004 (-0.025 to 0.032) in 2024: consistent with random mating.
  • Ne is not census size; with separate sexes LD estimates ran about 5-25% above the true Ne in validation, whereas the temporal estimate was within 6%; treat a single LD estimate as slightly optimistic.

Limits that matter

  • LD Ne overstated when the sample is a large share of the population: at S = 50, N = 120 planted-truth LD 1/Ne bias -13%/-12% (gate FAIL).
  • LD Ne overstated by 6-25% in separate-sex populations (independent verification); the simulator is monoecious, bull trout are not, so real LD bias may be larger. Temporal method unaffected.
  • Overlapping generations not validated: a single-sample LD estimate for bull trout approximates the number of breeders (Nb), not per-generation Ne.
  • Temporal method with < 3 generations between samples was outside module validation; this example's own recovery at 2 generations passes (bias +1.6%, coverage 0.92).
  • Temporal CI relies on a simulation-calibrated design effect; coverage 0.90-0.92 with 15-20 microsatellites (nominal 0.95).
  • With missing microsatellite data, LD Ne differs from NeEstimator by a median 1.7% (max 12%).
  • Half-sib misclassification is under-predicted by the simulated-pair table; treat second-degree calls as tentative.
  • Not validated: migration/structure, null alleles or genotyping error, selection. The module estimates Ne, never census N.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  • LD Ne overestimated in separate-sex populations (independent verification): +6-25% in Ne (1/Ne bias -6% to -20%) with no selfing, worst with 20 microsatellites, skewed sex ratio or S a large share of N; LD coverage 0.76-0.91. The temporal method is unaffected. Monoecious controls pass.
  • Ne overestimated when S << Ne. At S = 50 and Ne = 500, LD 1/Ne is biased -6% to -12% (Ne +7-14%; section 3, design grid). With 10 microsatellites and S = 25 the bias is large in either direction (grid: harmonic-mean Ne 39 at Ne 50, 267 at Ne 500). Report the interval, not the point estimate.
  • LD jackknife CI is slightly narrow with many SNPs: coverage 0.91-0.93 at the default design (1,000 SNPs), 0.89-0.93 at Ne 500. It passes the gate but is below nominal; the parametric CI is far worse and is not used. At S = 200 the grid used the parametric CI and its coverage with SNPs was 0.20-0.58 (not the module's default; the power run in section 7 uses the jackknife at S = 200).
  • Temporal CI relies on a simulation-calibrated design effect (constant 0.5) for correlated frequency change among loci in drift LD. Calibrated and tested only for unlinked loci, Wright-Fisher demography and 3-10 generations. With 15-20 microsatellites coverage is 0.90-0.905 (bound 0.885; nominal 0.95).
  • Missing data, microsatellites: our LD estimate differs from NeEstimator's by a median 1.7% (max 12%) in Ne because alleles are screened per locus rather than per locus pair (section 8). Not a bias in recovery, but results will not reproduce NeEstimator to the digit when data are missing.
  • Queller-Goodnight for unrelated pairs runs 7-13% more negative than the sample-relative truth (absolute 0.002); Lynch-Ritland (the default rel_primary) does not. QG passes everywhere except the large-Ne design.
  • Relatedness CIs for class means are conservative (coverage 0.94-1.00; SE 1.2-1.5x the empirical SD).
  • Half-sib misclassification is under-predicted by the simulated-pair table (section 9).
  • Not validated: overlapping generations (LD estimates then approximate Nb; Waples et al. 2014), migration/structure, linked SNPs (all simulations use unlinked loci; tightly linked SNPs will bias LD Ne downward), selection, the monogamy mating option, null alleles/genotyping error, the bottleneck test under stepwise mutation, and the temporal method with fewer than 3 generations between samples.
  • Simulations use Ne = N (ideal population); the module estimates Ne, never census N.

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated Wright-Fisher populations at the default design (100 for other designs), scored on heterozygosity loss per generation, 1/(2Ne), and relatedness class means; default PASSED all 9 estimands (bias at most 7.3%, coverage 0.910-1.000).
  • Harder design (15 microsatellites, 30 per sample, Ne 200, 3 generations, 5% missing) passed all 9 but gave very wide intervals; large Ne (500 with 50 per sample) failed 2 of 9, with Ne overestimated about 13%.
  • Negative controls failed as required: wrong sampling plan for the temporal method (-40% bias, coverage 0.04) and full-sib families left in the sample (LD +26%, coverage 0.18).
  • False-positive check at effectively infinite Ne: a finite upper Ne limit was claimed in 0/100 (LD) and 5/100 (temporal) replicates.
  • Cross-checks: identical to the NeEstimator v2 program on its own worked example, within 0.3% (SNPs) and a median 1.7% (microsatellites with missing data) on 20 simulated sets, and identical to Demerelate for Lynch-Ritland relatedness.
  • Design-precision grid and power runs over 25-200 individuals: once there are hundreds of SNPs, individuals rather than loci limit precision; at Ne 500, 25 per sample usually could not bound Ne.
  • A separate verifier agent with its own separate-sex simulator found LD Ne biased upward by 6-25% (temporal method unaffected), confirmed the default results exactly, and fixed several silent data-entry problems; verdict: verified with limits.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

All recovery runs: bin/Rscript core/run.R recover popgen-ne [--config modules/popgen-ne/validation/<cfg>.yaml] --nsim N --cores 16 --out results/popgen-ne/recover_<cfg> (launcher dev/popgen-ne/run_recover_all.sh), 2026-10-19, R 4.5.3, engine as in this folder. Recovery uses fast_settings (no simulated kin pairs, no Pcrit grid, no heterozygosity table; these do not change the estimates that are checked). Replicate r uses seed 100000 + r, so designs that differ only in the fitting or in one design knob share data sets. Gate (core recovery_gate, unchanged): |bias| <= max(10% of truth, 3 MC SE); coverage >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (floor 0.80); >= 90% of replicates "converged" (here: finite estimates). No threshold was changed.

Simulation model (simulate.R): Wright-Fisher population of N = Ne diploids (random union of gametes, selfing allowed, discrete generations), unlinked loci, founders in HWE/LE, 15 generations of burn-in. An "early" sample of S individuals, a "late" sample S individuals generations_between generations later (non-lethal: sampling plan I, census N known), and a kin panel of full-sib and half-sib pairs born in the late generation (not used for Ne). Kinship is tracked exactly through the pedigree; the relatedness truth for a pair is its pedigree relatedness re-expressed relative to the sampled group (the reference moment estimators use): r* = 2(theta_ij - theta_i. - theta_.j + theta_..)/(1 - theta_..), which is why the full-sib truth is 0.48, not 0.50.

Estimands checked (biological scale): het_loss_LD_<sample> and het_loss_temporal = 1/(2Ne), the proportion of heterozygosity lost per generation (truth 1/(2Ne)). This is the scale on which LDNe and the temporal estimators are nearly unbiased and on which the estimate stays finite when Ne is infinite; Ne itself is reported as 1/(2 het_loss) with the transformed interval. relatedness_<class>_<LR|QG>: mean r of the known pairs of each class.

Honesty notes.

  1. The temporal (Jorde-Ryman) CI was changed during validation. The first full run used NeEstimator's normal-approximation jackknife over loci. At the hard design (15 microsatellites) its coverage was 0.88 (bound 0.885: FAIL; 10/12 misses were intervals lying entirely below the truth). It was replaced by a chi-square interval on the uncorrected Fs with df = 2/CV^2 from the same jackknife (the construction NeEstimator uses for the LD jackknife CI), mapped to Fs'. All runs in this file were then repeated with the final engine; first-run outputs are kept in results/popgen-ne/v1_normalci_*.
  2. With hundreds of SNPs, every published temporal CI is far too narrow because allele-frequency changes at unlinked loci are correlated through drift LD in a small population. At the default design the jackknife SE was 1.6x too small (coverage of the plain jackknife interval about 0.79). The module inflates the jackknife variance by DEFF = 1 + 0.5 (D - D/L) R^2', with D independent alleles, L loci and R^2' the drift LD measured by the LD method in the same samples. The constant 0.5 was chosen on exploration runs (seeds 5001-5120, dev/popgen-ne/explore_var.R, logs/popgen-ne_explore*.log) and then tested on new seeds (sections 1-3, 5). The same DEFF scales the df of the Nei-Tajima and Pollak chi-square CIs.
  3. The bottleneck test was changed from BOTTLENECK's Wilcoxon signed-rank test to a Monte Carlo test after the size check showed 0.18 (100 data sets) / 0.128 (500) false rejections at equilibrium.

4. Negative controls (the gate can fail)

Both share seeds with the default design (identical early samples).

Mis-specified sampling plan (validation/misspec_plan2.yaml): data simulated under plan I (sampled individuals return and reproduce, census N = 100), but the census size is withheld, so the temporal method applies the plan II correction. het_loss_temporal: mean 0.00298 vs truth 0.005 (-40%), coverage 0.04 -> FAIL. LD and relatedness unaffected (PASS; het_loss_LD_early coverage 0.88 on these 100 replicates, just below 0.885; the same data at nsim 200 in section 1 give 0.91).

Family over-representation (validation/misspec_sibs.yaml): 20 full-sib pairs (40 juveniles of 20 families) are added to the late sample and used for Ne (use_for_ne not set). het_loss_LD_late +26.3% (Ne -21%), coverage 0.18 -> FAIL. The temporal estimate is barely affected (+3.4%, coverage 0.95). This is the Waples & Anderson (2017) situation; the module's use_for_ne column exists to avoid it.

5. False-positive rate (no drift signal)

validation/null_infinite.yaml: Ne = 10,000 (het_loss = 0.00005), S = 60, 1,000 SNPs. The relevant error is claiming a finite upper limit for Ne (CI for het_loss excluding 0 from above): 0/100 for LD early, 0/100 for LD late, 5/100 for temporal (nominal 0.025 one-sided, 0.05 two-sided; MC SE 0.022). Intervals entirely below 0 (no evidence of drift at all) occurred in 4/100 and 2/100 LD replicates. LD point estimates were infinite in 46-51% of replicates, as expected. The bias gate "fails" here (-73% / -81% of 0.00005, absolute -0.00004 in het_loss, i.e. a 0.1% error in E(r^2_sample)); it is irrelevant at this Ne and reported for completeness.

Harmonic-mean Ne over replicates (1/mean(1/Ne_hat)) is within 15% of the truth in every SNP cell (worst: 435 at Ne 500, S 25, 500 SNPs; 570 at Ne 500, S 50, 500 SNPs); with 10 microsatellites and S = 25 it ranges from 39 (Ne 50) to 267 (Ne 500). Rules of thumb from the grid: for CV <= 0.2, Ne = 50 needs S ~ 50 with 500+ SNPs (CV 0.10-0.11; 20 microsatellites give 0.22); Ne = 100 needs S ~ 50 SNPs-wise or S ~ 100 with 20 microsatellites; Ne = 500 needs S ~ 100 with SNPs or S ~ 200 with 20 microsatellites. More SNPs beyond ~500 help little: individuals, not loci, limit precision once there are hundreds of SNPs. The design_precision figure in every report shows this grid with the study's S marked.

The LD differences with missing data come from one documented choice: NeEstimator recomputes allele frequencies, the Pcrit screen and the number of independent alleles separately for every locus pair from the individuals typed at both loci, whereas this module screens alleles once per locus over all typed individuals and computes each pairwise r^2 from pairwise-complete moments. With complete data the two are identical (8Ne50). Recovery with 5% missing data (section 2) shows no bias attributable to this choice.

Relatedness (dev/popgen-ne/crosscheck_rel.R, 15 microsatellites, 300 pairs): Lynch-Ritland identical to Demerelate 0.9-3's lxy/lxy.w (max |difference| 1.1e-16). Queller-Goodnight: identical to a direct loop over the QG (1989) ratio-of-sums formula (3.3e-16); Demerelate's rxy averages per-locus ratios instead (a different estimator; correlation 0.953 with ours). Against an independent estimator family, hierfstat's Weir-Goudet beta (2 x beta.dosage), class means on one data set were full sibs 0.45 (QG 0.42, LR 0.29), half sibs 0.23 (0.21, 0.20), unrelated -0.034 (-0.043, -0.034), pedigree truth 0.47 / 0.23 / -0.021. The low LR full-sib mean on this single data set is estimator noise (LR is the noisiest with highly polymorphic loci): over 100 replicates of the hard design LR full-sib bias was -1.3%.

Analytic expectation. At drift-LD equilibrium E(r^2_drift) ~ 1/(3Ne) for unlinked loci (Hill 1981, the basis of Waples 2006): mean R^2' in the exploration runs was 0.0032 at Ne 100 (1/(3Ne) = 0.0033), 0.0016 at Ne 200 (0.0017), 0.0061 at Ne 50 (0.0067) and 0.00064 at Ne 500 (0.00067).

10. Bottleneck test

15 loci, 50 individuals, infinite-alleles data generated by the Hoppe urn (theta = 5). Rejection rate at P < 0.05: equilibrium 0.052 (500 data sets; MC SE 0.010); after 4 generations at Nb = 10 or Nb = 25 from an equilibrium population of 1,000: 1.00 and 1.00 (100 each). The Wilcoxon signed-rank test used by BOTTLENECK (Piry et al. 1999) rejected 0.128 at equilibrium, because He given k is skewed; the module reports it only for reference. Not checked: stepwise-mutation data (the test is known to be anti-conservative for microsatellites evolving by SMM under the IAM null).

Independent verification (2026-10-19, separate verifier agent)

Verdict: VERIFIED_WITH_LIMITS. The module recovers truth at the builder's default design (rerun reproduced exactly). It passes the gate for the temporal method and relatedness at independent designs. LD Ne is biased upward in dioecious (separate-sex) populations, which the builder's monoecious simulator could not reveal. The issue is documented in the report text and Known limits below.

Literature / code check. I compared the engine line by line with Waples (2006) / Waples & Do (2008), as implemented in strataG ldNe.R (fetched). E(r^2_sample) = 1/S + 3.19/S^2 (S >= 30) and 0.0018 + 0.907/S + 4.44/S^2. Ne = (a + sqrt(a^2 - b r'))/(2r') with (a, b) = (1/3, 2.76) or (0.308, 2.08). Monogamy uses (2/3, 7.2) and (0.618, 5.24). All are identical in engine.R (exp_r2_sample, ld_inv_ne). Jorde & Ryman (2007) Fs' with the plan I census terms, Ne = t/(2Fs'), and Nei-Tajima Fc / Pollak Fk also match (the builder also matches the NeEstimator C program exactly on 8Ne50). simulate() shares no code with fit() beyond the data structure. The independent simulator below shares none at all.

Controls.

  • Negative control (generation_time given as half the true value, i.e. the generations between samples are wrong) fails as required: temporal bias -49% to -53% in every design, coverage 0-0.52.
  • False positives: design N, an infinite population (no drift; 600 SNPs, S 40 x 2, plan II, nsim 200). A finite upper Ne limit (CI for het_loss above 0) was claimed in 0/200 (LD sample 1), 0/200 (LD sample 2) and 4/200 (temporal, 0.02). Two-sided rejection of het_loss = 0 occurred in 0.05 / 0.02 / 0.05 (nominal 0.05). LD point estimates were biased slightly negative (-3e-5, about 0.1% of E(r^2_sample)), harmless.
