Abundance

Distance sampling (conventional, multiple-covariate and hierarchical)

"How many deer (birds, primates, tortoises...) are there per square kilometre and in my study area, and is density higher in some habitats?" Observers miss more animals the farther away they are. Distance sampling models how detection falls with distance (the detection function), estimates the share of animals within the truncation distance that were seen, and corrects the counts for the ones that were missed.

module id: distance! Example verdict: OPENestimates accurate · intervals too narrow

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Observers walked line transects or stood at points and recorded the distance (perpendicular for lines, radial for points) to every animal or group they detected, with detection falling off with distance; the question is how many animals there are per km2 and in the study area, and (optionally) whether density is associated with habitat measured per transect. Exact or binned distances, single or repeated visits, groups (clusters), strata.

Not for

Counts without distances (use nmixture), detection/non-detection (occu-single), individually identified animals (scr or capture-recapture), camera-trap distance sampling with snapshot moments (not implemented), surveys where animals on the line are missed (g(0) < 1, e.g. diving whales or cryptic birds: needs double-observer mrds, not implemented), spatial density surface models over a full study area (DSM; only covariate-based prediction to a grid is offered).

The data you need

Observations CSV (transect id, distance, optional group size, optional visit, optional observer/weather), transects CSV (id, line length or number of visits, stratum, habitat covariates; every surveyed transect, including those with no detections), study-area size (km2, per stratum) for abundance, optional prediction grid.

Try it

The worked example

Mule deer density on a winter range from line-transect distance sampling, with shrub-cover and road-distance effects (simulated data)

The question: How many mule deer are on the 360 km2 winter range (per km2 and in total, overall and in the north and south units), and is deer density higher where shrub cover is higher and farther from the highway?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have a mule deer line-transect survey in this folder: a file of deer groups seen with the perpendicular distance from the line (m), group size, observer and date; a file of the 36 transects with their length, management unit (north or south, 180 km2 each), shrub cover and distance to the highway; and a 1 km2 grid of the whole 360 km2 winter range with the same two habitat values. Please estimate how many deer there are per km2 and in total, for each unit and overall, and tell me whether deer are more common where shrub cover is higher and farther from the road. Check that the method works for a survey like mine before you trust it, and give me a report and figures I can put in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • A group within 400 m of the line had probability 0.44 (95% CI 0.38 to 0.50) of being detected. Equivalently, the effective strip half-width is 175 m.
  • Density: 12.0 (95% CI 9.24 to 15.5) individuals per km2, which over the 360 km2 study area is 4,314 (95% CI 3,328 to 5,593) individuals.
  • This is 3.59 (95% CI 2.82 to 4.58) groups per km2 times a mean group size of 3.34 (95% CI 3.12 to 3.56).
  • By stratum: north 7.97 (95% CI 5.68 to 11.2) per km2; south 16.0 (95% CI 11.6 to 22.0) per km2.
  • Each increase of 10 percentage points in shrub cover multiplies expected density by 1.89 (95% CI 1.56 to 2.30), i.e. +89%.
  • Each increase of 3 km in distance from road multiplies expected density by 1.40 (95% CI 1.12 to 1.74), i.e. +40%.
  • This is an association from an observational survey, not proof that shrub cover causes the difference.
  • The hierarchical model's density over the study area (12.2 (95% CI 9.70 to 15.3) per km2) agrees with the design-based estimate above.
  • Goodness of fit: Cramer-von Mises test of the distances, p = 0.72. No evidence of lack of fit.

How we know it works

Planted truth at the example's design

Before trusting the example's results, the module was run on many simulated data sets with the same design and known true values. Each row is one quantity: did the method get it back? (What the thresholds are.)

Verdict for this example: ! OPEN

What OPEN means here. The module works (it passes at its default design), but at this example's design not every quantity passed the planted-truth check, so CascadiaTools does not certify those. 11 of the 13 quantities checked passed outright. For density at average site, group density at average site, the estimate itself was accurate (within 10% of the truth on average), but the 95% interval was too narrow, containing the truth only 88% to 89% of the time. Use those estimates, but read their intervals as somewhat over-confident. The quantities that passed can be used as reported. With your own data, the report would say the same and suggest what would help: more data, a simpler model or a different estimator. More on DONE and OPEN.

Why, for this example:

All estimands that answer the question (density, abundance, per-unit density, detection, habitat rate ratio) pass planted truth at this design, the fit converged and both GOF checks pass; but the HDS density at an average transect (whose uncertainty also sets the bands on the conditional-effect curves and the map) under-covers (0.885 vs bound 0.904; VALIDATION.md Known limit 9, prepared fix not installed).

Limits that matter

  • HDS intercept (density_at_average_site) ignores detection-key selection uncertainty: coverage 0.885 at this design (0.92 when half-normal chosen, as in this data set; 0.81 otherwise). Design-based density, abundance and slopes unaffected (Known limit 9).
  • Design-based density intervals are conservative when density follows habitat across transects (coverage 0.975 here; Known limit 3).
  • g(0) = 1, no evasive movement and accurate distances cannot be tested from these data; 75 m evasive movement biased density -27% in simulation, heaping at zero biased it upward (Known limit 4).
  • Recovery simulated one habitat covariate and a single detection scale; the second covariate (road) and the observer difference were checked only on the example fit.
  • The density map extrapolates to grid cells with shrub cover outside the surveyed 13.9-45.4 %.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  1. Fewer than ~40 detections: the HDS intercept (density_at_average_site) under-covers (0.89 at 8 lines); with binned point data and a hazard-rate truth, density is biased +14% and the hazard-rate shape is not recoverable (section 2b, 2c). The report warns when < 60 detections.
  2. gdistsamp (repeated visits) needs many transects and visits: +23% bias in density at 60 x 4, +5% at 120 x 6. Availability is weakly identified. If animals are sedentary between visits it fails badly (-29%); pool visits as effort with the single-visit engines instead.
  3. Design-based intervals are conservative (coverage 0.98-0.99) when density follows a covariate trend across transects (R2/P2 behaviour), so they are wider than necessary.
  4. g(0) = 1, no movement, accurate distances are untestable from the data alone: evasive movement gives -27% with coverage 0.45; heaping at 0 gives erratic upward bias (median +8%, 10% of datasets > 2x). How often the GOF test flags these violations was not measured.
  5. The bootstrap variance option is slow (10-15 s per resample) and was validated only at the 12-line design (100 replicates).
  6. MCDS detection-function figures show the curve averaged over covariates (no per-level curves).
  7. Not implemented: double-observer (mrds, g(0) < 1), density surface models (DSM), camera-trap distance sampling, availability for diving or singing animals other than via repeated visits.
  8. Observer-effect false-positive rate was 0.075 (15/200), borderline but within the binomial range.
  9. HDS intercept ignores key-selection uncertainty (found in independent verification, below): density_at_average_site (and density_per_km2_hds) come from the AIC-best HDS key only. When the truth is half-normal the hazard-rate wins AIC in 20-30% of data sets, and the intercept interval then covers the truth in only 70-79% of them. At a 70-point design with 2 pooled visits overall coverage is 0.873 (FAIL, bound 0.897). Design-based density, abundance and the habitat slopes are unaffected. A fix is prepared but NOT installed or validated (see below).

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated data sets at the default design using Distance and unmarked; all 8 quantities passed, with design-based density intervals somewhat conservative (coverage 0.985).
  • Variants passed with 100 replicates each: groups with strata and observer effects, binned point transects, and repeated-visit surveys at 120 transects x 6 visits.
  • Harder designs: 12 lines passed; 8 lines (about 38 detections) failed one interval (coverage 0.890); sparse hazard-rate point data biased density +14.2%; 60 transects x 4 visits biased density +22.6%.
  • Negative controls: evasive movement (density -27.3%), heaping of distances at zero and sedentary animals across visits all failed; with no true effect, false-positive rates were 0.055 (habitat) and 0.075 (observer).
  • Cross-checks on 50 data sets per scenario: Distance and a hand-written likelihood agreed to better than 1e-6; unmarked's habitat model matched to 3.5e-5.
  • Power: about 50 lines (about 240 detections) give 80%+ power for a 28% density change per SD of habitat (0.77 at 30 lines).
  • A separate verifier with its own simulator passed independent line and point designs, found the key-selection coverage problem (0.873 in one point design), and fixed point-survey effort and data-entry checks.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

All runs: bin/Rscript core/run.R recover distance [--config dev/distance/rc_<label>.yaml] --nsim N --cores 16 (SLURM, 16 cores, njobvu or chinook) with the module fast_settings (keys hn + hr, cosine adjustments up to 2 by forward AIC, AIC model averaging, analytic variance; HDS derived quantities from 500 MVN draws; no GOF bootstrap). Raw results: results/distance/recover_<label>/ (summary CSV, per-replicate CSV, HTML report). Final runs: 2026-10-20 (code version with the HDS P_a-CV screen, see section 8). Gate (core/R/recovery.R::recovery_gate, thresholds unchanged):

bias<= max(10% of truth, 3 MC SE); 95% interval coverage >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (floor 0.80);

>= 90% of replicates converged (likelihood engines: optimiser converged, finite SEs, link-scale SE < 5).

Default design (module defaults): 30 line transects of 3 km, each in a 3 x 2 km cell (study area 180 km2); groups placed by an inhomogeneous Poisson process, density 4 / km2 at the average cell, log-density slope 0.4 per SD of a habitat covariate; half-normal detection, sigma = 150 m; exact distances simulated to 600 m and truncated at w = 400 m; single visit, no groups. This gives a median of 145 detections within w (IQR 137-155). Engine auto = Distance::ds (density, abundance, detection) + unmarked::distsamp (habitat effect, density at an average site).

Timing: selftest (4 reps) about 10-20 s per replicate; 100 replicates take 2-4 min on 16 cores.

sigma_m is reported only when the AIC-best model is a half-normal without adjustments, so its n is smaller than for the other estimands; hr_scale_m/hr_shape likewise only for a plain hazard-rate.

8. Fix made during validation

The first round showed density_at_average_site in binned point transects (half-normal truth) with mean 107 vs truth 20: in 2 of 100 datasets the HDS chose a hazard-rate fitted as a near-spike to the 6 bands (P_a near 0, density 1,640 and 6,862 / km2) that passed the link-SE < 3 screen. The HDS engine now also drops a key whose average detection probability has CV > 50% when another key is stable (the rule Distance::ds candidates already had). Forcing the HDS to reuse the Distance::ds key was tried first and rejected: it made 7/100 point replicates non-converged (hazard-rate on 6 bins has a singular Hessian) and lowered default-design coverage of density_at_average_site to 0.895. All recovery runs that use unmarked were rerun after the fix (sections 1-4 and 6 are post-fix).

Independent verification (2026-10-20)

By a separate verifier agent. My own simulator, dev/distance/verify/indep_sim.R, uses no module code. It writes researcher-style CSVs plus a config, then runs read_data, check_data and fit (fast_settings) and scores the fits with core summarise_recovery / recovery_gate. Its designs and parameters differ from the builder's: unequal line lengths, raw-unit covariates, 2 strata, group sizes, points in square cells with positions drawn in the plane. Logs: logs/distance_verify_*.log, logs/distance_verify2_*.log. Results: results/distance/verify/.

Literature / code check. I checked these line by line against Buckland et al. (2001) and Fewster et al. (2009):

  • Half-normal and hazard-rate keys (Hayes & Buckland 1983 form), P_a for lines and points, ESW and EDR.
  • The R2/P2 encounter-rate variance.
  • Model-averaged SE (Buckland, Burnham & Augustin 1997).
  • unmarked's hazard "scale" taken as log shape.

The simulator places animals uniformly in the covered strip or disc (r = W sqrt(U) for points) and thins them by g(x). Truth P_a is the realised mean over animals inside w. The simulator and the fits share only ds_pa/ds_g, which the independent simulator does not use. No errors found.

The numbers reproduce the table in section 1 (density -1.6%, cov 0.985; HDS intercept -0.9%, 0.930; slope -1.2%, 0.920, power 0.995). Changes made by the verification:

  1. R/engine_hds.R (before the runs above):

    • Point surveys with effort other than 1 visit per point now enter the HDS with an offset log(effort). unmarked ignores tlength for points, so without it points2 gave the HDS intercept +108% (cov 0.10).
    • distsamp is also fitted from data-based starting values, keeping the better fit. With unmarked's default start, 8 of 60 point fits ended on sigma near 10 km with NaN SEs.

  2. R/io.R (usability):

    • A non-numeric distance, size or length (e.g. "85m", "1,20") previously crashed check_data with "missing value where TRUE/FALSE needed". It now stops with the column, rows and values and a fix ("write 85, not 85m").
    • A misspelled transect id now gets a "did you mean MD01?" hint.
    • Empty distances are reported with their row numbers.
    • A wrong column name lists the columns that were found.

  3. Prepared, not installed:

    • dev/distance/verify/engine_hds_v2.R and report_v2.R model-average the HDS derived quantities over the stable keys. They mix each key's MVN draws in proportion to Akaike weights, matching what the Distance::ds part already does.
    • The figures and interpretation then use the mixed draws.
    • This was not installed because a verifier job was still using the module and my shell access was revoked before I could run it.
    • Before use: copy both into R/, run selftest, then run_v2.sh points2:150 lines:200 null:200 and recover (default, rc_hard8, rc_point_binned_hn). The expected outcome is intercept coverage near 0.95.
