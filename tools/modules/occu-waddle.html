Occupancy

Two-species conditional occupancy (dominant/subordinate; Richmond et al. 2010, Waddle et al. 2010)

"Is the subordinate species (B) found at fewer sites where the dominant species (A) occurs than where it is absent, and is that pattern stronger in some habitats?", "Is B harder to detect on surveys when A is detected?", after allowing for both species being missed on some surveys.

module id: occu-waddle✓ Example verdict: DONE

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Repeated detection/non-detection surveys of TWO species at the same sites, where one species can reasonably be treated as dominant (predator, invasive competitor, larger species) and the question is whether the other (subordinate) species occupies sites less (or more) often where the dominant is present, and whether detection of one depends on the other; covariates on every occupancy and detection probability.

Not for

Three or more species, or no defensible dominant/subordinate ordering (use occu-rota, which is symmetric); counts or abundance of the dominant (abund-interactions); single species (occu-single); multi-season colonisation/extinction dynamics.

The data you need

Detection history of both species (site x survey 0/1, both recorded on every survey), site covariates, optional survey covariates.

Try it

The worked example

Do invasive Cuban treefrogs push out native green treefrogs? Two-species conditional occupancy from PVC-pipe surveys (simulated data)

A crew placed a cluster of 10 PVC pipes at each of 180 sites spread over wetlands and tree islands west of Miami (latitude 25.50-26.00 N, longitude 80.35-80.94 W). Each site was visited 5 times, about every 3 weeks, from 5 June to 31 August 2023. On each visit an observer checked all 10 pipes and recorded whether each species was found in any pipe (1) or not (0), and the air temperature. Some visits did not happen (flooded access, staff shortage): 60 of the 900 planned visits are missing.

The question: Is green treefrog occupancy lower at sites where Cuban treefrogs occur, and is green treefrog detection lower on visits when Cuban treefrogs are found in the pipes, after allowing for both species being missed?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have pipe-survey data for Cuban treefrogs and green treefrogs at 180 sites, 5 visits each, in pipe_surveys.csv, and site information (distance to road, canopy cover) in sites.csv. I want to know whether green treefrogs occupy fewer sites where Cuban treefrogs are present, and whether green treefrogs are harder to detect on visits when Cuban treefrogs are found. Cuban treefrog detection probably depends on air temperature. Please run the analysis and give me a report with figures I can use in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Where Cuban treefrog is present, green treefrog occupies 0.31 (95% CrI 0.21 to 0.43) of sites; where Cuban treefrog is absent, 0.67 (95% CrI 0.56 to 0.78).
  • The odds of green treefrog occupying a site are 0.21 (95% CrI 0.10 to 0.44) times as high where Cuban treefrog is present.
  • The species interaction factor is 0.64 (95% CrI 0.46 to 0.81): at average conditions the two species co-occur less often than expected if they were distributed independently (a segregation pattern).
  • Occupancy of Cuban treefrog: the odds are multiplied by 0.40 (95% CrI 0.26 to 0.57) per 1 SD of log(dist_road_m); it decreases from 0.96 at 29 m to 0.09 at 5410 m.
  • Occupancy of green treefrog where Cuban treefrog is absent: the odds are multiplied by 2.13 (95% CrI 1.35 to 3.63) per 1 SD of canopy cover (from 0.33 at 3% to 0.89 at 89%); where Cuban treefrog is present there is no clear change (1.14, 95% CrI 0.67 to 1.98).
  • Where both occur, green treefrog is detected on 0.23 (95% CrI 0.13 to 0.36) of surveys that detected Cuban treefrog and on 0.50 (95% CrI 0.36 to 0.64) of surveys that missed it; log-odds ratio -1.18 (95% CrI -2.01 to -0.40). This is a detection effect, not an occupancy effect.
  • A difference between psiBA and psiBa, or an SIF away from 1, is a co-occurrence pattern, consistent with competition or predation but equally with both species responding to habitat features not in the model; it does not by itself show that Cuban treefrog excludes green treefrog.

Limits that matter

  • Power for the detection interaction is modest: 0.61 for a log-odds ratio of -1.05 at 180 sites x 5 visits.
  • Canopy effects on green treefrog occupancy have low power (0.39 and 0.53); the psiBa slope magnitude is uncertain by about +-15% (VALIDATION.md sections 1, 8). In this data set the psiBA canopy slope was estimated at 0.13 vs true 0.60 (interval covers truth).
  • The dominant/subordinate direction is assumed (Cuban treefrog dominant); the model does not test it.
  • Co-occurrence is not interaction: shared unmeasured habitat drivers produce psiBA < psiBa (Blanchet et al. 2020).
  • A detection interaction (rBA < rBa) can come from shared survey conditions affecting both species, not only behaviour.
  • Small-sample bias toward 'no interaction' (psiBA, P(both), SIF) is documented at 60 sites; at this design (180 x 5) all were within +-2.5%.
  • Not validated: detection < 0.3, factor covariates, covariates on rBA/rBa (none used here).
All known limits from the module's validation record
  • Few sites (about 60): psiBA, P(both) and SIF are biased toward "no interaction" (+12% to +21%); report the direction, not the size, and see the power table before concluding "no effect". The ML engine fails on 10% of such data sets (boundary estimates).
  • Logit slopes inflate in magnitude at small n (a known small-sample property of logistic regression on partly latent states). The default Normal(0, 1) slope prior keeps the bias within 10% at 200 sites; with prior_sd: 2 the psiBa slope is exaggerated by 27%. At 60 sites slopes are unreliable with either prior.
  • psiBa habitat slope is exaggerated by 10-17% at 100-300 sites (power runs and section 1; each within about 2 MCSE, so the gate passes at 200 sites, but the direction is consistent). Treat its magnitude as an upper estimate.
  • Low power for the detection interaction: 0.59 at 200 sites for a log-odds ratio of -0.82; most studies cannot detect a detection interaction of this size.
  • Strong habitat effects on psiBa are shrunk by the default Normal(0, 1) slope prior: a slope of -1.5 per SD was recovered as -1.33 (+11%, gate FAIL on bias, coverage 0.97) at 200 x 4 (section 8). With prior_sd: 2 or ML it is exaggerated by 12-13% instead. Treat the magnitude of the psiBa slope as uncertain by about +-15%; its sign and interval are reliable.
  • 3 surveys per site: P(both) over-estimated by 12% even with no interaction (150 sites, section 8); other estimands pass and the false-positive rate is 0.025-0.055.
  • Direction is assumed: results are conditional on which species is called dominant; the module does not test the direction.
  • Co-occurrence is not interaction: shared unmeasured habitat drivers produce psiBA < psiBa (Blanchet et al. 2020); the gate checks estimation, not causation.
  • Not validated: low detection (< 0.3), 2 surveys (3 surveys: P(both) +12%, see above), factor covariates, survey covariates on rBA/rBa (a covariate on rA passed in section 8), long-format input in recovery (the reader is tested on the example only), model selection by AIC.
  • Goodness of fit uses posterior predictive checks of co-detection classes and per-site detection counts; their power to detect violated closure or heterogeneity has not been measured.

How this module was validated

  • 100 simulated data sets fitted with Nimble (3 chains x 2500 iterations) at the default design; all 18 quantities passed, with coverage (how often the 95% interval contained the truth) 0.91-0.98.
  • A harder 60-site design failed 3 of 18 (interaction quantities biased +12% to +21% toward no interaction); a wider slope prior (SD 2) also failed one slope, so SD 1 became the default.
  • Negative control: ignoring detection failed 7 of 10 occupancy quantities; with no true interaction, false-positive rates were 0.045-0.055 at 200 sites.
  • Cross-checks: the Nimble likelihood matched a hand-written one to 4.5e-13; the ML engine matched wiqid::occ2sps and unmarked::occuMulti log-likelihoods exactly.
  • Power: about 150-200 sites x 4 surveys give 80-95% power for an occupancy interaction of this size; habitat effects on conditional occupancy need well over 300 sites.
  • A separate verifier with its own simulator passed a facilitation design (120 sites x 6) 18 of 18, found narrow failures in a 3-survey design and a strong-habitat design, reproduced the builder's run exactly, and fixed text-detection handling and report wording.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

All recovery runs used core/run.R recover (simulate -> fit -> compare with truth) and recovery_gate() with the fixed core thresholds: |bias| <= max(10% of |truth|, 3 Monte Carlo SE) (for truth 0: max(0.1 x empirical SD, 3 MCSE)); coverage of 95% intervals >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (0.885 at nsim 100, 0.904 at nsim 200); >= 90% of replicates converged (Nimble: max R-hat < 1.05; ML: optimiser converged, invertible Hessian, no |logit| > 7). Seeds are seed * 100000 + rep. Outputs: results/occu-waddle/<run>/; configs: dev/occu-waddle/*.yaml; table script: dev/occu-waddle/mdtable.R.

Default design (defaults in module.R): 200 sites x 4 surveys, 5% of surveys missing. One standardised site covariate habitat on all three occupancy probabilities and one standardised survey covariate effort on pA and pB. Truth at average covariates: psiA 0.5 (slope 0.8), psiBA 0.3 (slope 0.5), psiBa 0.7 (slope -0.5), pA = pB = 0.5 (effort slopes 0.4), rA 0.5, rBA 0.35, rBa 0.55. Derived: psiB 0.5, P(both) 0.15, SIF 0.6, occupancy log-odds ratio -1.69, detection log-odds ratio -0.82.

Recovery settings (fast_settings): Nimble 3 chains x 2500 iterations (500 burn-in), per-coefficient slice samplers, posterior median as point estimate, equal-tailed 95% CrI; no predictive check. Real-data default is 6000 iterations (1000 burn-in) and requires R-hat < 1.01 and bulk ESS >= 400.

Priors (default since this validation): intercepts Logistic(0, 1), slopes Normal(0, SD = 1) on standardised covariates. Section 1b shows why SD = 2 was abandoned.

1b. Same design with prior_sd = 2 - FAIL (1 of 18)

Archived in results/occu-waddle/_old_prior2/recover_default (same seeds): 17/18 pass, but the slope of subordinate occupancy where the dominant is absent fails on bias: truth -0.5, mean posterior median -0.637, bias -0.137 (MCSE 0.040; tolerance 0.120), relative bias -27%. Coverage was fine (0.95). The ML estimator on the same 100 data sets has the same direction of bias (-0.074, -15%, passes only because its tolerance is 3 MCSE = 0.117), so this is a finite-sample property of the logit slope estimated from roughly 100 dominant-absent sites in which B's state is partly latent, not a coding error; the vaguer prior lets the posterior's long tail pull the median further out. With Normal(0, 1) the bias is -0.051 (-10%), power for that slope drops from 0.47 to 0.40. Choice of the SD = 1 default is documented in README (Gelman et al. 2008; Northrup & Gerber 2018); users can set prior_sd: 2 as a sensitivity check.

Posterior mean instead of median (prior SD 2, recover_default_postmean): also fails the same slope (-34%), so the median was kept.

Failures (all bias, coverage fine 0.92-0.95): psiBA +17%, P(both) +21%, SIF +12% (true 0.6, mean 0.67). With few sites where both species occur (about 9 of 60) the Logistic(0,1) intercept prior and the small-sample behaviour pull psiBA up toward 0.5, so the estimated interaction is weaker than the truth (SIF too close to 1, occupancy log-odds ratio -1.65 vs -1.69 is fine on average but very imprecise, CI width 4.3). ML engine at 60 sites (recover_hard60_mle): 10 of 100 fits hit a boundary (|logit| > 7) or a singular Hessian and are flagged non-converged (the report says so); on the remaining 90 every estimand passes the gate, but slopes are inflated (+27% psiA slope, -47% psiBa slope, +42% psiBA slope; tolerances are wide because MCSE is large). The excluded 10% are the hardest data sets, so ML at 60 sites is not better than Nimble; it fails visibly instead of silently.

3. Negative control: ignore detection (naive engine) - FAILS as it should

engine: naive (logistic regression on "ever detected"), default design, nsim 100: 7 of 10 occupancy estimands FAIL. psiBa -10% (coverage 0.58), P(both) -21% (coverage 0.78), psiA coverage 0.76, psiB coverage 0.84, psiA slope -11%, psiBA -13%, psiBa slope +14%. 7. Selftest

Timing from the selftest was used to size the 100-replicate runs (median 44 s per fit on 16 cores; about 6 min per 100 replicates).

8. Independent verification (2026-10-19, separate verifier agent)

Scripts: dev/occu-waddle/verify/ (indep_sim.R is the verifier's own simulator and shares no code with the module: it draws the latent site state from one 4-category draw and, at "both" sites, each survey's (yA, yB) pair from one 4-category draw; covariates are raw units, elevation in m and day of year, which the module must standardise itself). run_verify.R runs run_recovery() with the module's fit() and the core recovery_gate(). Outputs: results/occu-waddle/verify/<scenario>/; logs: logs/occu-waddle_verify_*.log. Seeds 7 * 100000 + rep.

Literature check. Parameter definitions, default ties (psiBA -> psiBa, rA -> pA, rBa -> pB, rBA -> rBa) and the conditioning of rBA / rBa on A being detected on that survey match wiqid::occ2sps (Richmond et al. 2010 parameterisation) line by line; the site likelihood (4 latent states, with states that contradict a detection contributing 0) matches the MacKenzie et al. (2004) / Richmond et al. (2010) form in both the R and the Nimble code. SIF = psiBA / psiB = psiAB / (psiA psiB) (MacKenzie et al. 2004); logit(psiBA) - logit(psiBa) equals the log-linear interaction alpha_AB of MacKenzie et al. (2021) / Rota et al. (2016). On a verifier-simulated data set (scenario A, 8 free parameters, elevation on all three occupancy probabilities) the module's ML engine and wiqid::occ2sps give the same log-likelihood (-500.9075) and the same coefficients to 4 decimals.

Rerun of the builder's default recovery (core/run.R recover occu-waddle --nsim 100 --cores 16, verify/rerun_default): identical to section 1 to 4 decimals for every estimand (same seeds), 18/18 PASS, 100/100 converged, max R-hat 1.004.

Reading: the module recovers truth at a design and parameter values it was not tuned on (including facilitation, SIF > 1, a positive detection interaction, a survey covariate on rA and raw-unit covariates). The psiBa slope (B's habitat response where A is absent, estimated from sites where B's state is partly latent) is the weakest estimand: ML and a vague prior exaggerate it by 12-15%, the default Normal(0, 1) prior shrinks a large one (|1.5| per SD) by 11%. No prior choice makes it pass at both 0.5 and 1.5; the default errs toward zero, which is the conservative direction, and coverage stays at 0.95-0.97. With only 3 surveys, P(both) is over-estimated by 12% even with no interaction, the same small-sample direction as at 60 sites. The false-positive rate at 150 x 3 is 0.025-0.055.

Usability (messy-data test, dev/occu-waddle/verify/messy/test.R). Wrong species column name, misspelled site id (WCA-0O2), duplicated row, count instead of 0/1, an NA for one species on a done survey, a missing survey covariate, a misspelled covariate in a formula and a site with no surveys all gave specific, actionable messages. One real problem was found and fixed: detections typed as text (yes/no, ?) were silently turned into NA, producing misleading messages ("never detected", "180 sites have no surveys").

Report check (example config refit, results/occu-waddle/example): every number in the interpretation was recomputed from the posterior draws (odds ratios = exp of the reported log-scale estimates and limits; range sentences; SIF and both log-odds ratios) and matches; wording separates detection from occupancy effects, uses the probability scale and makes no causal claim. Figures: conditional occupancy (psiBA vs psiBa along canopy), SIF, detection interaction, odds ratios, per-covariate conditional effects on the probability scale with 95% bands, PPC and traces.
