Regression and causal inference

GLM / GLMM with biological interpretation

"How does Y change with X?", where Y is something you measured on each sampling unit:

module id: glm✓ Example verdict: DONE

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

One response measured on sampling units (sites, plots, nests, animals, quadrats, surveys) and the question is how it changes with habitat, environmental or treatment predictors, possibly with repeated measures within sites/individuals/years (random intercepts). Counts, presence/absence (0/1), successes out of trials, proportions in (0,1), positive continuous measurements (mass, length, biomass) and normal measurements.

Not for

Imperfect detection that must be separated from occurrence or abundance (use occupancy / N-mixture modules); spatial or temporal autocorrelation that must be modelled explicitly; random slopes, nested (1|a/b) syntax, splines/GAMs, ordinal or multinomial responses; causal effect estimation (use the causal/SEM modules); proportions containing exact 0s or 1s (transform first or use binomial with trials).

The data you need

One CSV, one row per observation, with a response column, predictor columns, optional grouping column(s) (site, individual, year), optional effort column (offset) and, for binomial counts, a trials column.

Try it

The worked example

Wood Thrush point counts: negative-binomial GLMM with survey-effort offset and station random intercepts (simulated example)

Wood Thrush (Hylocichla mustelina) is a forest-interior songbird of eastern North America. It nests in the understorey of closed-canopy, mostly deciduous forest, and it has declined over much of its range. A field crew wants to know where the birds are most numerous, so that managers know which stands to protect.

The question: Does the number of Wood Thrushes counted change with canopy cover, elevation and forest type?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

I have Wood Thrush point counts in wood_thrush_counts.csv: 45 stations, each counted 4 times in May-June 2025. Most counts were 10 minutes, some were 5. I want to know whether the number of thrushes changes with canopy cover, elevation and forest type. Please analyse it and give me a report with figures I can use in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Per 10 percentage points of canopy cover (%) this is associated with a 39% higher expected Wood Thrush count (rate ratio 1.39, 95% CI 1.18 to 1.64).
  • Across the observed range of canopy cover (%) (42 to 98), with other continuous predictors at their mean; forest type = deciduous; typical station, the expected Wood Thrush count goes from 0.432 to 2.78.
  • Per 100 m of elevation (m) this is associated with a 5.1% lower expected Wood Thrush count (rate ratio 0.949, 95% CI 0.854 to 1.05). The 95% interval includes no effect: the data are compatible with effects from 31% lower to 13% higher, including none.
  • Compared with forest type = deciduous (reference), forest type = conifer is associated with a 52% lower expected Wood Thrush count (rate ratio 0.482, 95% CI 0.248 to 0.935): 0.528 vs 1.1.
  • Compared with forest type = deciduous (reference), forest type = mixed is associated with a 15% higher expected Wood Thrush count (rate ratio 1.15, 95% CI 0.651 to 2.01): 1.25 vs 1.1. The 95% interval includes no effect: the data are compatible with effects from 35% lower to 101% higher, including none.
  • Model checks: DHARMa simulated residuals showed no detectable problems with distribution, dispersion, outliers or zeros (all p >= 0.05).
  • These are effects on the expected count as recorded, which combines how many/whether animals are present with how likely they are to be detected or recorded.

Limits that matter

  • At 45 stations x 4 visits with ~1 bird per count, the station random-intercept SD is biased -18% (23% of simulated fits singular) and NB theta is sometimes unbounded: neither is interpreted (this run).
  • Rate ratios for imprecise contrasts are biased upward in the mean (exp of a noisy log ratio): +7.2% conifer, +4.9% mixed here; log-scale coefficients unbiased (Known limit 1).
  • Wald intervals only; recovery coverage 0.905-0.940 at this design (Known limit 4).
  • Expected counts are for a typical station (random effect 0), not the landscape mean (Known limit 5).
  • Counts confound abundance and detection; a canopy effect could partly be detectability (N-mixture module separates them).
  • Spatial autocorrelation among stations is not modelled (Known limit 7).
  • Associations, not causal effects.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  1. Odds/rate ratios for imprecise contrasts are biased upward in the mean. exp(beta-hat) is median-unbiased but not mean-unbiased: E[exp(b)] is about exp(beta + SE^2/2). When the SE of a log ratio exceeds about 0.35 (factor contrasts in Bernoulli GLMMs; small samples), the ratio estimand fails the mean-bias gate: +13% (binomial_glmm habitat), +15-90% in the small designs. The link-scale coefficient passes and the ratio's 95% CI keeps nominal coverage. Report ratios with their CIs; do not average point ratios across studies.
  2. Few groups. With 10 sites x 4 visits (NB GLMM) Wald intervals for site-level effects under-cover (0.875-0.88) and the site SD is biased -42%. With 15 Bernoulli sites x 4 the site SD interval under-covers (0.845). The module flags fewer than 5 levels; between 5 and ~15 groups, treat site-level effects' intervals as somewhat optimistic and run a planted-truth check at the researcher's design. Power below ~20 sites is low for site-level effects in any case.
  3. NB size theta needs data. With 100 observations and 30% structural zeros, theta is biased +29% (coverage 0.87). Overdispersion is then hard to separate from zero inflation; the coefficients and zero-inflation probability are still recovered.
  4. Wald intervals. All intervals are Wald (log scale for SDs and dispersion). Profile or bootstrap intervals are not implemented. For random-effect SDs near zero the interval is meaningless and the report says the fit is singular.
  5. mean_response_at_reference is for a typical group (random effect = 0), not the population average.
  6. Not validated: random slopes (rejected by the parser), non-default links (probit, cloglog, inverse; accepted but their interpretation sentences are generic), zero-inflation formulas other than ~1 (supported, zi_beta_* reported, not recovery-tested), polynomial terms (supported in fitting and plots, not recovery-tested), the stats::glm fallback engine (cross-checked to agree, not separately recovery-tested), and DHARMa test calibration (the tests come from DHARMa, unmodified).
  7. Spatial/temporal autocorrelation is not modelled or tested for.

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated data sets per design (500 for false-positive checks), fitted with glmmTMB using REML for mixed models; all 200 converged at the default design and every one of 12 quantities passed.
  • Nine model families (Gaussian, binomial, Poisson, beta, Gamma, zero-inflated and interaction models) each tested with 200 replicates; all passed except odds ratios for factor contrasts in the binary mixed model (+13% mean bias, a known back-transformation effect).
  • Harder small designs failed as expected: 10 sites x 4 visits (coverage 0.875-0.88, site SD -42%), 15 binary sites x 4 visits, and a small zero-inflated design (theta +29%).
  • Negative controls: with no true effect, intervals excluded zero 5.8% and 4.2% of the time (nominal 5%); fitting Poisson to overdispersed data or dropping the site random effect failed the gate, as it should.
  • Cross-checked on 20 data sets per case against lme4, stats::glm, MASS::glm.nb and hand-written likelihoods; coefficients agreed to within 0.14 standard errors at most.
  • Power: about 40 sites give 80% power for a site-level effect of 0.3 per SD (power 0.74 at 30 sites, 0.93 at 50).
  • A separate verifier agent reran the builder's runs exactly, passed three new designs with its own simulators, confirmed the controls fail, and fixed an offset-matching bug and several report-wording issues.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Explained differences:

  • SE differences of about 6% for glm.nb and glm(Gamma) arise because glm uses expected information and, for Gamma, the Pearson dispersion. glmmTMB uses the observed Hessian at the ML shape.
  • NB2 GLMM vs glmer.nb: coefficients differ by up to 0.14 SE (median 0.11 SE). In dev/glm/nb_ll_check.R the glmmTMB objective at the glmmTMB MLE is higher than at glmer.nb's estimates in all 20 datasets, by 0.006 to 0.024 log-likelihood units (results/glm/validation/nb_glmm_loglik_check.csv). The module therefore reaches the better optimum. glmer.nb's own reported log-likelihood differs from glmmTMB's by -0.06 to +0.12, so the two packages evaluate this likelihood differently; glmer.nb fits theta in an outer loop. The difference is small next to the SEs.

ML vs REML (why REML is the default for mixed models)

An earlier full suite used ML (results/glm/validation_ml/, same configs). Under ML, re_sd_site was biased low in every mixed design: -11.9% default, -11.6% Gaussian, -17.9% Bernoulli, -9.6% beta, -8.3% Gamma, -10.1% ZIP, -17.3% ZINB. Group-level contrasts were under-covered in the ZIP/ZINB GLMMs (0.880-0.890 < 0.904), and the null false-positive rate for the site-level covariate was 0.084. Under REML (started from the ML solution) all of these pass: re_sd within +/-3%, ZI coverage 0.915-0.95, false-positive rate 0.058. With glmmTMB's default start, REML stopped at a spurious zero-SD optimum in 7/200 beta GLMM datasets. Restarting from the ML estimates reached a better REML optimum (lower objective, positive-definite Hessian) in all 7, so the module always starts REML from ML. If REML still fails to converge, the ML fit is reported with a note.

Independent verification (2026-10-18, separate verifier agent)

Scripts: dev/glm/verify/ (verify_recovery.R = own simulators + driver, run_verify.sh, rerun_after_fix.sh, usability.sh, messy/). Outputs: results/glm/verify/. Log: logs/glm_verify.log. Final code hashes: dev/glm/verify/code_md5_verified.txt.

Literature / parameterisation check. Engine assumptions were checked against glmmTMB 1.1.15.2 ?sigma.glmmTMB and Brooks et al. (2017): NB2 Var = mu(1 + mu/theta) with betadisp = log(theta); beta Var = mu(1-mu)/(1+phi) (Ferrari & Cribari-Neto 2004); Gamma sigma = 1/sqrt(shape) = CV, so the module's log CV = -0.5 x betadisp is right; zero inflation is a logit-link mixture with E[y] = (1-pi) mu. The module's simulator shares canon_family, link_inv and coef_label with the fitter and standardises with the sample SD exactly as the fitter does, so I did not reuse it.

Independent planted truth. My simulators (no module code) set truth on the RAW covariate scale and derive the per-SD estimands analytically (beta_SD = beta_raw x sample SD; intercept at the sample mean; for x:factor interactions, factor contrast = b2 + b3 x mean(x)). The per-unit estimands (*_per_<u>) are therefore checked against raw-scale truth that does not depend on the scaling code. Problems found and fixed (module files only; core untouched).

  1. Offset partial matching (engine.R, module.R). sp$offset used R's partial $ matching: if the spec had no offset element but had offset_per, the value of offset_per (e.g. 1 or 10) was taken as the offset column, so log(df[[1]]) (often the response) entered as an offset. In my first smoke test this made every Gamma GLMM fit collapse (slopes 0, CV 1e-7) and every binomial-trials fit fail. Through read_data() the same happened when a config had offset_per: without offset: (stopped with a confusing "column(s) 10 not found"). Now exact [["offset"]] lookups, plus a clear check_data message for offset_per without offset. The builder's suite was not affected (its simulator always sets offset).
  2. Misspelled group ids (module.R). WT03 vs WT-03 was silently fitted as a 46th station. check_data now stops with "grouping column station has labels that look like the same station spelled differently: 'WT-03' (3 rows) vs 'WT03' (1 rows) ..." (case, punctuation and leading zeros ignored). fit notes also flag single-observation groups when the typical group has >= 3.
  3. Figure export (output.R). The figure named "Effect of canopy cover (%)" failed to save in core export_figures() (% in the file name; ggsave treats it as a format string). The figure list names now spell out % and /. The underlying core issue (file stem = figure name, unsanitised) remains for other modules.
  4. Interaction sentences (output.R). They read "Each 1-SD increase (or, for a factor, the named level instead of the reference) in forest_type_conifer multiplies the rate ratio for canopy_cover_pct by 0.922", with raw column names. Now: "when forest type = conifer rather than deciduous, the rate ratio per 1 SD of canopy cover (%) (15.6) is multiplied by 0.922 (95% CI 0.49 to 1.74)". Identity-link and continuous x continuous versions were checked too.
  5. Detection caveat (output.R). For count and 0/1 families the interpretation now says that effects on recorded counts/detections combine abundance/occurrence with detectability, and points to occupancy / N-mixture modules. Not added for successes/trials, beta, Gamma or gaussian.
  6. Missing-value message now says "data rows N, not counting the header line".
