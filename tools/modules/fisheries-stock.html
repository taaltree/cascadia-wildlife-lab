Fisheries

Fish stock assessment - catch curves, stock-recruitment and surplus production

Fisheries questions about mortality, recruitment or stock status. (1) catch_curve - aged fish from a survey or fishery sample; question is total mortality Z / annual survival of fully recruited fish. (2) stock_recruit - a brood table of spawners and the recruits they produced; question is productivity, density dependence, S_MSY, U_MSY, steepness. (3) surplus_production - an annual catch series plus an abundance index (CPUE or survey); question is MSY, B/B_MSY, F/F_MSY (Kobe status).

module id: fisheries-stock! Worked example: OPEN

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Fisheries questions about mortality, recruitment or stock status. (1) catch_curve - aged fish from a survey or fishery sample; question is total mortality Z / annual survival of fully recruited fish. (2) stock_recruit - a brood table of spawners and the recruits they produced; question is productivity, density dependence, S_MSY, U_MSY, steepness. (3) surplus_production - an annual catch series plus an abundance index (CPUE or survey); question is MSY, B/B_MSY, F/F_MSY (Kobe status).

Not for

Length-only data (convert to age with an age-length key first, or use a length-based method), separating fishing from natural mortality (needs an M estimate or tagging), growth curves (use fisheries-growth), age-structured assessment (stock synthesis, SAM), catch-only methods without an index, stocks whose index is not proportional to biomass (hyperstability), recruitment series with errors-in-spawners problems, one-way-trip catch/CPUE histories for production models (weakly identified, see Known limits).

The data you need

Catch_curve - one row per aged fish (age) or a catch-at-age table (age, count). stock_recruit - brood year, spawners, recruits. surplus_production - year, catch (every year), abundance index (may have gaps)..

The worked example

Walleye catch curve: total annual mortality from fall gill-net ages (simulated)

The question: What is the total annual mortality (and survival) of adult walleye in the lake?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

What the researcher typed

Here are otolith ages from our fall walleye gill-net survey (walleye_fall_gillnet_ages.csv). I want to know the total annual mortality and survival of adult walleye. Please do the analysis and give me a report with figures I can use.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Total instantaneous mortality of fully recruited walleye (ages 3 and older) is Z = 0.53 per year (95% CI 0.33 to 0.72; weighted regression).
  • Equivalently, about 59% (48% to 72%) of fully recruited fish survive each year and 41% (28% to 52%) die from all causes combined (fishing plus natural mortality).
  • The other estimators give Z = 0.50 (0.33 to 0.66; Chapman-Robson) and Z = 0.47 (0.33 to 0.61; Poisson-lognormal GLMM). All estimators agree within their uncertainty.
  • The age distribution is much more variable than the smooth decline expected from constant mortality and recruitment (Chapman-Robson c-hat = 2.97). This usually reflects variable year-class strength.
  • Treat the interval as closer to a 90% interval.
  • Z mixes fishing and natural mortality; separating them needs an independent estimate of natural mortality M (F = Z - M).

Limits that matter

  • Catch-curve 95% intervals under-cover when year-class strength varies: coverage 0.900 at this design (gate bound 0.904), 0.84-0.93 across validation designs; read as ~90% intervals.
  • Chapman-Robson (-4.6%) and GLMM (-6.8%) under-estimate Z at this design; weighted regression bias -0.5%.
  • A recruitment trend biases Z (negative control +29%); dome-shaped selectivity and under-ageing of old fish also bias Z.
  • Z is total mortality; F = Z - M needs an independent natural-mortality estimate.
  • Data are simulated.

Engine, references and full details

Engine: catch_curve - FSA::catchCurve (weighted regression) + Chapman-Robson (hand code, checked against FSA) + glmmTMB Poisson-lognormal GLMM; stock_recruit - least squares (lm / optim) or nimble (Bayesian, AR(1) residuals); surplus_production - nimble (Bayesian state-space, Meyer & Millar 1999).
