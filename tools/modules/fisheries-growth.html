Fisheries

Fish growth (von Bertalanffy) and length-weight condition

Individual age-length data (otolith/scale ages) for one or more groups (populations, lakes, sexes, years); question is how fast fish grow, how large they get, at what age they approach maximum size, whether growth or condition differs among groups, or whether growth varies with a group-level covariate (temperature, density, year).

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

Individual age-length data (otolith/scale ages) for one or more groups (populations, lakes, sexes, years); question is how fast fish grow, how large they get, at what age they approach maximum size, whether growth or condition differs among groups, or whether growth varies with a group-level covariate (temperature, density, year). Optional weights give the length-weight relationship and condition (Wr or Kn).

Not for

Length-frequency data without ages (needs ELEFAN/mixture methods), tag-recapture growth increments (Fabens model), back-calculated lengths from annuli (repeated measures per fish), stock assessment / mortality (use fisheries-stock), growth models other than von Bertalanffy (Gompertz, logistic, Richards are not implemented).

The data you need

One row per fish with age (years), length, group (optional), weight (optional); optional group-covariate table (one row per group).

Do female and male walleye differ in growth and condition? (simulated autumn 2014 gill-net survey)

Results from the example data

What this means

  • female: fish approach an asymptotic length (Linf) of 685 mm (95% CI 665 to 706) and grow toward it at K = 0.302 (95% CI 0.272 to 0.335) per year. They reach 90% of Linf (about 617 mm) at age 6.6 (95% CI 6.0 to 7.2) years.
  • male: fish approach an asymptotic length (Linf) of 561 mm (95% CI 547 to 575) and grow toward it at K = 0.385 (95% CI 0.339 to 0.437) per year. They reach 90% of Linf (about 505 mm) at age 4.8 (95% CI 4.3 to 5.4) years.
  • Linf in male is smaller than in female by 124 mm (95% CI 99 to 149). K in male is larger than in female by 0.084 per year (95% CI 0.025 to 0.142).
  • Kimura (1980) likelihood-ratio tests: common linf chi-square = 81.7, df = 1, p < 0.0001; common k chi-square = 8.3, df = 1, p = 0.0041; common t0 chi-square = 0.4, df = 1, p = 0.51; all parameters common chi-square = 448.5, df = 3, p < 0.0001.
  • Length-weight (1000 weighed fish): W = 1.19e-06 x L^3.340, with b 95% CI 3.308 to 3.372; this is positive allometry.
  • At the same length, fish in male weigh 2.1% more than fish in female (95% CI +0.9% to +3.3%).
  • Relative weight Wr by group: female 90.2 (95% CI 89.5-91.0, n = 530); male 91.1 (95% CI 90.4-91.9, n = 470).

Limits that matter

  • Linf needs old fish: 19 females and 54 males are beyond the age at 90% Linf here (enough); with < 5 such fish Linf is unreliable.
  • Length-selective gear (e.g. gill nets) biases K (-14%) and t0 (-0.36 y) and drops coverage to 0.58; selectivity is not modelled (simulated data have none). Drop partly selected ages with real data.
  • Single-parameter differences have low power: at this design power is 0.81 for a 0.08 difference in K and 0.10 for a 0.1-year difference in t0; a non-significant t0 test means not detectable, not equal.
  • Not modelled: ageing error, individual growth variation beyond the residual, growth models other than von Bertalanffy.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  1. Linf needs old fish. With few fish near the asymptote (hard_nls, hard_nimble_fixed) Linf, and so age at 90% Linf, is not identified: nls occasionally returns absurd Linf with wide intervals (age at 90% Linf fails the gate); the Bayesian model returns Linf biased +16 to +26% toward its prior. The module notes when < 5 fish are at or beyond the estimated age at 90% Linf; treat Linf as unreliable then and compare groups by length at ages that were sampled.
  2. Length-selective gear biases growth (section 4): K -14%, t0 -0.36 years, coverage down to 0.58. Selectivity is not modelled; drop partially selected ages.
  3. Single-parameter group differences have low power (section 6): 0.59 for a 50 mm Linf difference with 250 fish per group. Use Kimura's joint test or whole-curve comparisons.
  4. Hierarchical SDs with few groups. At 10 groups the posterior median of the among-group SDs is biased +6 to +7% (passes); the posterior mean was +14% (failed). With 6 groups x 40 fish sd_log_Linf is biased +24.5% and FAILS the gate (section 5): with fewer than 10 groups (independent verification: 8 groups x 70 fish also FAILS, +14.7% and +16.2%), interpret the among-group SD of Linf through its upper 95% limit ("variation in Linf is at most about N%"), not as a point value. nlme's REML gives smaller SDs than the Bayesian median on the example data.
  5. Hierarchical convergence needs longer chains than the fixed-group model: on the example data (12 groups, 2106 fish) 10,000 iterations gave max R-hat 1.010 and min bulk ESS 335 (t0), short of the real-data standard, so the hierarchical default is 20,000 iterations with 5,000 burn-in.
  6. Not modelled: ageing error, individual growth variation beyond the residual, Linf-K correlation among groups, growth models other than von Bertalanffy, length-at-capture date beyond age_offset.

How this module was validated

  • 500 simulated datasets at the default design with nonlinear least squares, and 100 with the Bayesian Nimble engine (3 chains x 3,000 iterations); both PASSED all 25 estimands (nls bias at most 0.8%, coverage 0.928-0.968).
  • Hierarchical model with 10 groups x 80 fish passed all 69 estimands after switching to posterior medians for the among-group SDs; with 6 groups (and 8 groups in independent checks) the among-group SD of Linf failed (+24.5% and +14.7%).
  • Harder design with few old fish (60 per group, high mortality): Linf was not identified in some datasets, and age at 90% Linf failed the gate.
  • Negative controls failed as required: length-selective gear (12 of 25 estimands failed, K -14%), unmodelled ageing error (13 of 33 failed), and Gompertz data fitted as von Bertalanffy (4 of 12 failed).
  • False-positive rates of the Kimura likelihood-ratio tests were 0.039-0.057 with identical growth (nominal 0.05); about 6-7% with a small second group of 90 fish.
  • Power over 30-250 fish per group: single-parameter differences need many fish, while Kimura's joint test had power 0.996 at 150 per group.
  • Cross-checks matched FSA, fishmethods, nlme and lm; two separate verifier agents with their own simulators (different units, group numbers and age structures) passed and reproduced the builder's runs; verdict: verified with limits.

5. Harder designs: where the module stops working

hard_nls: few old fish (60 fish/group, Z = 0.9; about 0.1 fish per group at age >= 8). 494/500 fits (6 errors: "nls fit failed from every starting value", reported to the user as such). 23/25 estimands pass the gate, but this hides the real problem: with almost no fish near the asymptote, Linf is not identified in a minority of data sets. Median Linf_A is 596 (truth 600) but 6.3-7.1% of fits put Linf above 1.5 x truth (mean Linf_A 2,380 mm; the bias passes only because 3 MC SE is then enormous), the median 95% CI is 56-68% of Linf wide, and age at 90% Linf FAILS (coverage 0.907 and 0.909 < 0.921; mean 55 vs 7.2 years, driven by those fits). Condition, length-weight and the K/t0 estimates pass. The module flags this situation in every fit (note when < 5 fish are at or beyond the estimated age at 90% Linf).

hard_nimble_fixed: same design, Bayesian fixed groups (97/100 converged). 9 FAIL: Linf biased +16 to +26% (posterior mean 722-757 vs 600-650), K_C -12.5%, t0 -21 to -31%, age at 90% Linf +33 to +59%; coverage stays 0.92-0.97. When the data do not identify Linf the posterior follows the prior N(log max length, 1), which is wide and skewed upward. Condition, length-weight, differences between groups and the residual SD pass.

hard_hier: hierarchical with 6 groups x 40 fish, Z = 0.6 (15,000 iterations, 5,000 burn-in). 97/100 reps converged (the first attempt at the fast setting of 3,000 iterations converged in only 44/100, so this design needs longer chains; 57 s per fit). 44 of 45 estimands PASS; sd_log_Linf FAILS: posterior median 0.0996 vs truth 0.08 (+24.5%, MCSE 5.8%), coverage 0.948. sd_log_K passes (+5.7%, coverage 0.959). Linf_typical +1.4% (coverage 0.969), K_typical -0.1%, t0 -1.1%, Linf_beta -0.8% (power 0.04), K_beta -6.0% (power 0.13); group-level Linf_g, K_g, age at 90% Linf all pass. With 6 groups the data barely inform the among-group SD of Linf, and the half-normal(0, 0.5) prior pulls it upward; the 95% interval is still honest. With 6 groups the covariate effects have power <= 0.13 for effects of this size.
