Movement

Hidden Markov models for animal movement (behavioural states)

"When is my animal resting/foraging and when is it travelling, how much time does it spend in each mode, and is switching between modes associated with time of day and habitat?"

module id: movement-hmm! Example verdict: OPENestimates accurate · intervals too narrow

Is this the right analysis?

Use it when

GPS / Argos-quality-filtered telemetry with fixes at a (roughly) regular interval, and the question is which movement modes an animal switches between (e.g. encamped/foraging vs travelling), how much time it spends in each (activity budget), and how time of day or habitat at its location is associated with switching between modes.

Not for

Habitat selection / resource selection (use movement-ssf); home-range size (movement-ssf home ranges / ctmm); fixes so irregular or error-prone that a regular grid is meaningless (fit a continuous-time model first, e.g. crawl multiple imputation); accelerometer or dive data (possible with momentuHMM but not built in here); individual random effects on state switching; hidden semi-Markov (non-geometric dwell times).

The data you need

One CSV with one row per fix - animal id, timestamp, coordinates (lon/lat or projected x/y in metres) - plus optional covariate columns measured at each fix (e.g. % forest cover, distance to road).

The worked example

Behavioural states of GPS-collared elk with a hidden Markov model (simulated example)

The question: Do collared elk switch between an encamped (short-step) and a travelling (long-step) movement mode, how much time do they spend in each, and is switching associated with time of day and forest cover?

The data are simulated, so the true answer is known and the analysis can be checked against it.

Here is a CSV of hourly GPS fixes from 5 elk (elk_gps.csv, times are Pacific time). I want to know whether they switch between resting and travelling, how much time they spend in each, and whether time of day and forest cover affect switching. Please analyse it and give me a report with figures I can use in a paper.

What the report says

Results from the example data

In plain language

  • Encamped (short steps): mean step 60 m per 1 h (95% CI 57-63 m), turning concentration 0.50 (0.43-0.57), i.e. little directional persistence (frequent sharp turns and reversals).
  • Travelling (long steps): mean step 454 m per 1 h (95% CI 434-473 m), turning concentration 2.32 (2.17-2.49), i.e. moderate directional persistence.
  • Activity budget (expected long-run share of time in each state, averaged over the 24-h cycle, at the average value of the habitat covariates): Encamped (short steps) 58% (95% CI 55-61%); Travelling (long steps) 42% (95% CI 39-45%).
  • Time of day: the probability that an animal is in Travelling (long steps) is highest around 11:00 at 71% (95% CI 67-76%) and lowest around 23:00 at 15% (12-19%), with habitat at its mean.
  • Each 1-SD increase in forest_cover_pct (14.9 units) multiplies the odds of switching from Encamped to Travelling (versus staying) per time step by 0.67 (95% CI 0.58-0.79); switching back from Travelling to Encamped changes by a factor 1.48 (1.26-1.76) per SD.
  • The model-predicted long-run share of time in Encamped (short steps) would be 41% (95% CI 36-47%) if forest_cover_pct were held at 32.5 (10th percentile of the fixes) and 73% (69-77%) if it were held at 72.8 (90th percentile). This is an association with the covariate measured at the animal's location, not proof that forest_cover_pct causes the behaviour.
  • States are statistical summaries of movement; labels such as 'resting/foraging' or 'travelling' are interpretations that should be checked against independent observations.

Limits that matter

  • Number of states is the analyst's choice; too few states merge behaviours and bias every parameter (VALIDATION.md Known limit 1).
  • Transition-coefficient intervals are slightly narrow: null false-positive rate 0.07-0.08 vs 0.05; at this design tpm_2to1_tod_sin covered 0.890 (Known limit 3).
  • Missed fixes (5.1%) are treated as missing at random; habitat- or behaviour-dependent fix failure can bias the encamped share and forest association.
  • All animals share one set of parameters (no individual random effects, no semi-Markov dwell times) (Known limit 6).
  • Habitat effects on switching are associations with conditions at the fix, not causal effects (Known limit 9).
  • State labels (encamped/travelling) are interpretations of step length and turning, not observed behaviours.
All known limits from the module's validation record
  1. Number of states. A model with too few states converges cleanly but merges behaviours and biases everything (section 5). The module does not choose N for you; AIC tends to over-select states in real data (Pohle et al. 2017). Compare 2 vs 3 states with pseudo-residuals and biology.
  2. Rare transitions in 3-state models can sit on a boundary (4% of replicates); their coefficients and the 3->1 time-of-day effect failed coverage narrowly (0.879). Interpret 3-state models through activity budgets / stationary probabilities.
  3. Transition-coefficient intervals are slightly narrow: false-positive rate 0.07-0.08 at nominal 0.05 (0.069 pooled over 1596 null tests in four null studies, section 11); coverage of covariate slopes 0.92-0.98; one time-of-day coefficient at 0.889 with 2-h fixes (section 10).
  4. Short tracks (~450 steps in total): time-of-day coefficients biased up to +17%; forest power ~0.37; 4% of fits have a singular Hessian. Weakly separated states: forest power ~0.43 and borderline coverage for the longer-step state's mean.
  5. Only gamma step lengths (with an automatic zero-mass when zero steps occur) and von Mises turning angles with mean 0 are built in. The zero-mass branch passed one recovery study (independent simulator, 6% / 1% zero steps; section 10).
  6. Pooled parameters across animals: no individual random effects; no hidden semi-Markov dwell times.
  7. regularise: crawl uses a single best crawl prediction, ignoring location uncertainty (no multiple imputation). On a strongly irregular test set it shortened steps by 10-18% and doubled turning concentrations (section 9); the angle pseudo-residual test flags it. Treat it as a last resort; for irregular data use MIfitHMM outside the module. Fix interval is estimated as the most common gap between fixes and snapped to a standard schedule; set data: interval_h if the collar schedule is unusual.
  8. momentuHMM's reported log-likelihood is offset by ~0.1-0.8 units relative to moveHMM because it applies Gamma(1) to the initial distribution (delta is the distribution one step before the first fix; sections 6 and 10). Harmless for estimation, but never mix engines in AIC tables.
  9. Habitat effects on switching are associations with conditions at the fix, not causal effects.

How this module was validated

  • 200 simulated datasets at the default design, fitted by maximum likelihood with momentuHMM (4 starting values); PASSED all 16 estimands (bias at most 2.8%, coverage 0.924-0.990), re-run with the final code at 200/200 converged.
  • Harder designs: a 3-state model passed all 36 estimands on re-run; short tracks (3 animals x 150 fixes) and weakly separated states each failed one switching coefficient (bias about +17% or +16%).
  • Negative control: fitting 2 states to 3-state data failed 12 of 16 estimands (step means +38% and +131%), showing the gate can fail even when fits look numerically healthy.
  • False-positive control with no forest effect passed; pooled over four null studies the interval excluded zero in 0.069 of 1,596 tests (nominal 0.05).
  • Cross-check against a hand-written likelihood and moveHMM: all 14 parameters agreed within 0.014 standard errors.
  • Power over track length: about 400 hourly fixes per animal (6 animals) needed for 80% power on the forest effect; at 100 fixes slopes were biased up to 19%.
  • Two separate verification passes with independent simulators (2-hour fixes with daylight saving; 30-minute fixes with two covariates) passed 15/16 and 18/18; the verifiers fixed the activity-budget definition and a spurious no-standard-error bug; verdict: verified with limits.

Every run uses the acceptance gate described on the validation page. The full record follows, section by section, exactly as kept in the module's VALIDATION.md.

Settings and pass/fail rule used for every run

All runs 2026-10-18/19, R 4.5.3, momentuHMM 1.5.8, moveHMM (cross-check only), on njobvu/chinook via bin/ejob with 16 cores. Reproduce everything with bash dev/movement-hmm/run_validation.sh (configs in modules/movement-hmm/validation/, outputs in results/movement-hmm/validation/<run>/; re-runs after the section-10 activity-budget fix in results/movement-hmm/verify/postfix_<run>/). Recovery uses fast_settings (4 starting values, 1000 parameter draws, no Viterbi/pseudo-residuals). Replicate r uses seed 100000 + r.

Gate (core recovery_gate, unchanged): |bias| <= max(10% of |truth|, 3 MC SE); 95% coverage >= 0.95 - 3 MC SE (floor 0.80); >= 90% of replicates converged. "Converged" = nlm code 1-3 and an invertible Hessian (otherwise no intervals exist; such replicates are excluded from bias and coverage and counted as failures in the convergence criterion). This is a likelihood module, so there is no R-hat; the "R-hat < 1.05" requirement of the build brief is replaced by this rule.

Simulation model (simulate_tracks): correlated random walk with gamma steps and von Mises turns on a smooth 20 x 20 km % forest surface; switching depends on cos/sin of the hour and the forest value at the animal's location; 5% failed fixes and +/-2 min fix-time jitter, so the grid regularisation code is exercised in every replicate. Truth for the transition intercept is expressed at the covariate mean used by fit(); the activity-budget truth is computed from the true parameters with the same function used for the estimate.

2. False-positive rate (forest effect = 0; 200 replicates)

All 16 estimands PASS (coverage 0.915-0.975). The 95% interval of the forest slope excluded 0 in 16/199 (0.080) replicates for 1->2 and 14/199 (0.070) for 2->1; pooled 30/398 = 0.075 (binomial 95% CI 0.05-0.11) against a nominal 0.05. Coverage of these slopes was 0.920 and 0.930: the Wald/delta intervals of transition coefficients are slightly too narrow (empirical SD 0.0056 vs mean reported SE 0.0051-0.0054, about 4-8% too small). Treat a forest effect whose interval only just excludes zero with caution; the report says so in Known limits.

3. Three-state model on three-state data (100 replicates)

Resting / foraging / travelling (step means 30 / 200 / 700 m, kappa 0.3 / 1.0 / 4.0), 6 animals x 1000 fixes, 36 estimands. Median 656 s per replicate (more starts would be slower still: 3-state fits are ~10x the 2-state cost). 35/36 PASS (re-run after the activity-budget fix, section 10; earlier text said 34/35, a miscount). All state-dependent parameters: |rel. bias| <= 0.7%, coverage 0.90-0.99. Activity budgets: bias <= 0.001, coverage 0.919-0.960 (bound 0.884). Forest slopes 1->2 / 2->1: rel. bias -5.7% / -0.5%, coverage 0.94 / 0.96, power 0.92 / 0.90.

Failure: tpm_3to1_tod_sin coverage 0.879 vs bound 0.884 (bias +0.065, truth 0). The 3->1 transition (travelling directly to resting) is rare (truth intercept -3): in 4/99 replicates its intercept went to the boundary (estimates -6.2, -10.1, -102, -695), with huge or no SEs. The mean bias of tpm_3to1_intercept (-1.19) and tpm_3to1_tod_cos (+1.03) is driven entirely by these replicates (median intercept -3.09 vs truth -3.0); they pass the bias rule only because the MC SE is large. Rare transitions in 3-state models are poorly identified; their coefficients must not be interpreted when their SE is large. Interpretation should be via activity budgets and stationary probabilities, which pass.

8. Worked example

elk_gps.csv is simulated (5 elk x 720 hourly fixes, lon/lat WGS84 in the Oregon Coast Range, local timestamps, failed fixes absent; generator dev/movement-hmm/make_example.R, truth in example_truth.rds). Fitted with the default settings (10 starts; 10/10 reached the same optimum; pseudo-residual KS p = 0.93 (steps), 0.75 (angles); lag-1 ACF -0.02). 15 of 16 95% intervals contain the truth (e.g. forest 1->2 -0.0265 [-0.0365, -0.0160], truth -0.025; activity budget encamped 0.582 [0.549, 0.611], truth 0.586; re-fitted 2026-10-19 after the section-10 fix, report results/movement-hmm/example/movement-hmm_report.html). The miss is the travelling turning concentration, 2.32 [2.17, 2.49] vs 2.5, consistent with the 95% nominal rate.

9. Irregular fixes

Test set dev/movement-hmm/crawl_test/ (built by dev/movement-hmm/make_crawl_test.R): 3 of the example elk, 30% of fixes deleted and every fix time jittered uniformly +/-25 min around the hourly schedule (an extreme, Argos-like irregularity; GPS jitter is usually seconds to minutes). Same truth as the example. One dataset only, so these are smoke tests, not a recovery study.

Bug found and fixed here. The fix interval was taken as the median gap between fixes; with 30% lost fixes this gave 77 min for a 60-min schedule, so every step was predicted over 1.28 h and the step means were inflated (crawl: 71 / 472 m). The interval is now the most common gap, snapped to a standard schedule within 10% (guess_interval() in hmm_engine.R), and the report states the value used. Checked: 60 min on 5 simulated default datasets, 60 min on the example and on this test set, 4 h on simulated 4-h data with 30% missing. Conclusion: for GPS data with small timing jitter use the default grid; for strongly irregular data neither option here is adequate; use multiple imputation (momentuHMM MIfitHMM) outside this module.

10. Independent verification (2026-10-19)

A second pass by a separate author, using scripts in dev/movement-hmm/verify/ and outputs in results/movement-hmm/verify/. Findings and what changed:

(a) Activity-budget definition fixed. The activity budget was the average of the hour-specific stationary distributions (as in moveHMM's plotStationary). With time of day on the transitions the chain is periodic, and that average is not the long-run share of time. It is now the periodically stationary distribution (Koslik et al. 2025) averaged over the fixes of one day, on the actual fix schedule (interval_h, first-fix phase), in periodic_probs() / activity_budget() in hmm_engine.R; the same function gives the simulation truth, the stationary_tod curve and the habitat curves. dev/movement-hmm/verify/periodic_check.R checks it against a brute-force simulation of the state chain (log logs/movement-hmm_verify_periodic.log): the new definition matches the simulated long-run shares to <= 0.001 in all three test cases (default 0.5648 vs simulated 0.5648; 3-state 0.333/0.377/0.291 vs 0.332/0.378/0.291; strong daily cycle 0.5401 vs 0.5403), whereas the old average was off by up to 0.018 (3-state state 1: 0.351). The default, null, 3-state and negative-control studies were re-run after the fix (results/movement-hmm/verify/postfix_*); sections 1, 2, 3 and 5 report the re-run numbers. Only the activity-budget rows changed (default coverage 0.944 -> 0.924; all other estimands identical to the pre-fix run because the fits are the same). The harder designs (section 4) and the power run (section 7) were not re-run; their activity-budget numbers use the old definition, and their listed failures concern other estimands.

(b) Independent simulator (indep_sim.R, no module code): 2-h fixes, timestamps in local time with daylight saving (America/Denver), an elevation covariate (m) instead of forest, failed fixes both as missing rows and as rows with blank coordinates/covariate, +/-6 min jitter, the fix interval NOT passed (the module must guess it), uniform initial state; 4 animals x 900 fixes; step means 120 / 900 m, kappa 1 / 4; elevation slopes +0.0025 / -0.003 per m.

11. Independent verification, second pass (2026-10-19/20)

A further verifier (scripts dev/movement-hmm/verify/v2_sim.R, v2_run.R, dev/movement-hmm/verify3/, dev/movement-hmm/verify4/; outputs results/movement-hmm/verify3/, verify4/, verify5/; logs logs/movement-hmm_verify{3,4,5}_*). This pass was interrupted once and resumed; all numbers below are from runs made with the current code (module code last changed 2026-10-19 13:59, output.R text-only changes afterwards).

Literature / model check. The model (gamma steps with mean/sd parameterisation, von Mises turns with mean 0, multinomial-logit transitions with staying as reference, covariates of row t driving s_{t-1} -> s_t, working parameters log/log/log/logit) matches Langrock et al. (2012) and the momentuHMM conventions. Rather than relying on documentation, the likelihood was re-implemented by hand (verify4/hess_check.R): it reproduces momentuHMM's log-likelihood to 1.2e-10 on 32 data sets, and the SEs from momentuHMM's nlm Hessian agree with a Richardson-extrapolated numDeriv Hessian of the hand-written likelihood to within 0.1% (median ratio 1.0000). So the slightly narrow transition-coefficient intervals (Known limit 3) are a finite-sample property of Wald intervals, not a numerical Hessian error. The simulator (simulate_tracks) shares only tpm_from_eta/tod_cols with fit-side code; tpm_from_eta's cell order was checked against momentuHMM's trMatrix_rcpp (section 10c), and the independent simulators below share no module code at all.

(a) Bug fixed: spurious "no standard errors" (in hmm_draws(), hmm_engine.R). When the initial-state distribution delta was on a boundary (e.g. every animal starts in the same state), the full Hessian is singular but solve() sometimes "succeeded", returning a numerically rank-deficient inverse; chol() then failed and the replicate was reported as having no intervals (~8% of fits in the second independent design; 2/200 at the default design, 8/200 in short tracks). The module now checks positive definiteness with a relative eigenvalue tolerance and conditions on delta (a nuisance parameter) when it is on a boundary, and also on zero-step probabilities estimated at ~0 (one zero step in the data switches the zero-mass branch on for all states). A note in the report says so. After the fix: default 200/200 converged (was 198), short tracks 200/200 (was 192), weak states 200/200 (was 196).
