Why It Needs Support

Better Data, Better Decisions

Biodiversity is changing faster than we can measure it. The decisions that shape forests, rivers and coasts still rest on evidence that is sparse, slow and incomplete, and the animals at the center of those decisions are often the hardest to find.

Closing that gap takes methods as much as fieldwork: a neural network that can find an owl in two million hours of sound, a laboratory protocol that can count animals from what they leave behind, statistics that turn detections into numbers a manager can use. Grants and contracts usually pay to answer one question. They seldom pay to build the tools that make the answer possible, or to share those tools once they exist.

Every capability described on this site, from the genetics lab to the AI models to the analytical methods, exists because someone funded its development. Partners and supporters make the next set of hard-to-study species and hard-to-answer questions tractable.

What Support Has Built

  • PNW-Cnet, a neural network that recognizes 84 species by sound, with the Pacific Northwest Bioacoustics Lab
  • Genetic methods that identify species, diets and individual animals without capture
  • CascadiaTools: 29 analysis modules, free to any agency, tribe or scientist
  • Bird Distribution Maps for 610 species, now in beta
  • NJOBVU-AI, open-source software for training camera-trap classifiers
